alfred Tasks · Refinement spec

ALF-161 — Add support for due times

A task can carry a time as well as a date — “Tomorrow 3 PM” — set in the due-date picker or read from a capture, and it turns red the minute that time passes.

Decisions

  1. D1How do you enter the time?

    A · an “Add time” field — phone wheel, typed on desktop ★ · B · four quick slots plus Other…

    A is one plain control everywhere; B saves taps but guesses your four times.

  2. D2Where does the time live?

    Its own wall-clock column, due_time; 3 PM stays 3 PM in any time zone.

    Every date-only path stays untouched, and a schema shape is costly to reverse.

  3. D3When does a timed task turn red?

    At its minute, live — no reload. Untimed tasks keep today’s rule.

    A time is a deadline; colouring it any later hides the one thing it adds.

  4. D4Where do untimed tasks sort within a day?

    After that day’s timed tasks — untimed means “by end of day”.

    Then the sort and the red/amber colouring tell the same story.

  5. D5What happens to the time on edits?

    Date moves and repeats keep it; clearing the date clears it; time-only dates today.

    Nothing silently drops a time you set, and no time floats without a day.

  6. D6Does the classifier read times?

    Yes — “dentist tomorrow at 3pm” lands as Tomorrow 3 PM.

☞ open: your pick · → proposed: my call · ✓ settled: yours. Merging accepts every proposed row.

Not doing

Reminders or notifications at the due time; an “Every hour” repeat; times on weekly-plan items.

Working detail

Everything below is for the implementing agent. Approving needs only what’s above.

Context

The ticket is the title alone, “Add support for due times”; the owner asked for this refinement to start over from the current code, ignoring an earlier one. Scope answers from the refinement session: times only — no reminders; the classifier extracts stated times; the engine’s hidden hourly repeat stays hidden.

What exists. items.due_date is a timestamptz (0001), but the whole system treats it as a calendar date: a bare YYYY-MM-DD is written, Postgres stores UTC midnight, and parseDueDate (frontend/lib/date-utils.ts) reads that back as local midnight of the same date. Code routinely does due_date.slice(0, 10) (task-row-menu.tsx, addDays’ contract), the classifier writes YYYY-MM-DD (workers/src/verdict.ts), and the corrections log compares to_char(due_date at time zone 'UTC', 'YYYY-MM-DD') (0029). Everything time-relative is day-granular: isDueDateOverdue / isDueToday / isDueTodayOrOverdue drive the due chip’s red/amber/blue (due-date-chip.tsx), the folder badges (useFolderBadgeCounts in tasks-store.tsx), the overdue-subtask tally (countOverdueDescendants, lib/tree.ts) and Today’s membership (rankDueToday, lib/tasks/due-today.ts); ordering uses Date.parse(due_date) in ownKey (lib/priority.ts), which both By-Priority’s tiebreak and the urgency-first compareKeyByDue (Today, folder “Due date” sort) read.

The picker is the month-grid Calendar atom inside a Radix popover, opened from the due chip (row and detail panel) and from ⋯ → Due date → Custom…; a day pick saves and closes. Recurrence (lib/recurrence/engine.ts) computes the next date and complete_and_spawn (0006) copies an explicit column list onto the next occurrence. The engine has an hourly frequency, marked “engine-only — needs a time anchor”, that writes a zone-less datetime into due_date; no UI reaches it.

alfred has no notification channel (no web push, no service worker), which is why reminders are a separate story.

Decision records

D1How do you enter the time? · open

A ★ — an “Add time” field. Under the grid, a Time row: “Add time” (clock + blue link, the footer buttons’ treatment) until a time exists, then a native <input type="time"> styled as the Input atom plus a × that clears just the time. Native is the point: iOS Safari opens its time wheel, desktop browsers give a typed, arrow-steppable hh:mm field, and there’s no list to build or keep accessible. The field commits on blur or Enter (not on every keystroke, or typing 3:30 would save 3:00 first) and Escape reverts.

B — quick slots. Four one-tap buttons (9 AM · 12 PM · 3 PM · 6 PM) drawn like the grid’s selected day, plus “Other…” that reveals A’s field; tapping the selected slot clears it. Faster for those four times, but it’s more chrome and the four are a guess about your day.

Either way a day pick keeps today’s save-and-close, and a time commit leaves the picker open — so “time, then day” is one visit and “day, then time” is two. Considered and rejected: staying open after a day pick once the time row is touched, a mode with nothing on screen to say you’re in it. The control lives in a new picker component both entry points share (D11).

D2Where does the time live? · proposed

Chosen: items.due_time time (no zone), nullable, meaningful only beside a date — a wall-clock “floating” time, the same way due_date is already a floating date. 3 PM means 3 PM on whatever device you’re holding, across DST and travel.

Rejected: (a) a real instant in due_date plus a has-time flag — every .slice(0, 10) and the corrections log’s UTC to_char go wrong for evening times west of UTC (7 PM CDT is tomorrow in UTC), and the Worker would need the owner’s zone to write one; (b) a floating time packed into due_date, midnight meaning “no time” — no migration, but midnight becomes unrepresentable and the column lies about being an instant.

Consequence: time-aware code reads two fields; all date-only code is untouched. The hourly engine path is left as is — still unreachable; whoever exposes it moves it onto due_time.

D3When does a timed task turn red? · proposed

A timed task is past due once the clock reaches its minute; an untimed one keeps today’s rule (red from the next day). The surfaces re-evaluate on a 60-second clock (useNow, D9), so the flip lands within a minute without a reload. Rejected: keeping colour day-granular and using the time only for label and sort — the label would say 9 AM while the chip still said “not late”.

Consequence: before its time a task due today is amber; at its time it turns red, moves from the folder’s amber attention count to its red overdue count, and starts counting in its parent’s overdue-subtask tally. Today’s membership stays date-based (a 9 AM task due today is in Today all day).

D4Where do untimed tasks sort within a day? · proposed

The sort key becomes the moment a task falls due: date + time, or the last millisecond of the day when untimed. Rejected: untimed first (the calendar “all-day” convention), which reads as most urgent while D3 says it’s the least late. Consequence: the priority tiebreak now separates only tasks sharing a moment, so in Today a Low task at 5:30 PM sits above a High untimed one (Plate II); By-Priority still leads on level, with a finer due tiebreak.

D5What happens to the time on edits? · proposed

  • Any date change — a grid day, the picker’s Today, the ⋯ presets (Today / Tomorrow / Next week) — keeps the time.
  • Clearing the date (picker Clear, ⋯ No due date) clears the time too; so does any type change that clears the date.
  • Setting a time on an undated task also sets the date to today (as the Repeat chip already stamps today as its anchor).
  • Completing a recurring task carries the time onto the next occurrence; spawned subtasks keep their own date and time, as their dates do now.

Rejected: resetting the time on a date move (a silent loss) and disabling time on undated tasks (an extra step for no safety).

D6Does the classifier read times? · settled

Owner’s call in this session. Rules, in the prompt’s precision-over-recall voice: answer due_time only for a stated clock time (“at 3”, “3pm”, “15:30”, “noon”); never from a part of day (“tonight”, “this afternoon”); a time with no stated day means today; a time is never written without the date it was guessed with (D10).

Implementation decisions

D7 · The database keeps a time from outliving its date

A CHECK (due_time is null or due_date is not null) plus a before update of due_date trigger that nulls due_time when due_date goes from set to null. The trigger is the backstop for writers that clear the date without knowing the time exists — enter_code_module, convert_to_code_epic, future RPCs — so none of them needs redefining. It fires only on that transition, so a time sent for an undated row still fails the CHECK loudly instead of vanishing. No change to items_task_only_fields: time ⇒ date ⇒ task already.

D8 · The wire format is 24-hour HH:MM

The API accepts due_time as HH:MM (z.iso.time({ precision: -1 }), nullable). PostgREST returns a time as HH:MM:SS, so every reader normalises with .slice(0, 5) and no comparison is string-vs-string across the two forms. Minute precision only.

D9 · One minute-clock, only where it can change something

The chip’s band and the two tallies take now from useNow(60_000). Keep timers off rows that can’t flip: read the clock in a small timed-chip child rendered only when dueTime !== null, and in useFolderBadgeCounts once for the whole map; the row’s overdue tally reads it only when its subtree holds an active timed task. Today’s ranking needs no clock (D4’s key is fixed).

D10 · The classifier writes a time only with its date

validateVerdict keeps due_time only when it is a valid HH:MM and due_date survived too. mergeIntoItem writes it only when the item has no time and either the merge is writing due_date in the same PATCH or the item already holds that same date — never a guessed time onto a different, human-chosen day. Validate in code rather than with a schema pattern/format (JSON Schema’s format: time is RFC 3339 with seconds and offset); check the anthropic-api skill for which keywords structured outputs honour. PROMPT_VERSION 4 → 5.

D11 · A shared DueDatePicker; the Calendar atom stays a month grid

New components/tasks/due-date-picker.tsx composes Calendar with the D1 time row; DueDateChip’s popover and the ⋯ menu’s Custom… popover both render it in place of a bare Calendar. The time row sits between grid and footer, so the picker needs a footer slot or a sibling layout — the implementer picks the smaller change to Calendar.

D12 · Labels use the device’s clock format

Intl.DateTimeFormat(locale, { hour: 'numeric', minute }), with minute omitted on the hour: en-US gives “3 PM” and “9:30 AM”, a 24-hour locale gives “15” and “09:30”. The chip label is `${formatDueDate(date)} ${formatDueTime(time)}`.

Ordering

The due key of every comparator (ownKey().due) is dueMoment(date, time): the local instant of date + time, or of the day’s last millisecond when untimed (D4); undated stays Infinity. That changes Today, the folder “Due date” sort and the By-Priority tiebreak together; their effective-key rollups (a parent floats on its most urgent active subtask) are unchanged.

API validation

  • POST /api/items: due_time requires a non-null due_date in the same body → else 400.
  • PATCH /api/items/[id]: due_time non-null together with due_date: null → 400. A time sent alone for an undated row reaches the CHECK; the UI never sends one.
  • Malformed time (3pm, 25:00, 15:00:00) → 400.

Classifier

  • Schema: due_time joins the required list as string | null; the system prompt names seven fields and gains the D6 rule beside due_date’s.
  • “today” for a time-without-day is the reference date the prompt already supplies (CLASSIFIER_TIMEZONE).
  • classified_guess records due_time; the live-verdict patch (classifierVerdictPatch) carries it to an open tab.
  • A human editing due_time claims the row, like due_date; the dispatch-time diff logs a due_time correction (compared as HH24:MI).

Edge cases

  • A 12:00 AM time is legal and distinct from untimed (D2): it is past due from that minute, and sorts first in its day.
  • Display-only chips (Today and By-Priority rows, select mode) show the time and flip colour too; they just can’t be edited.
  • A DST-gap time (2:30 AM on spring-forward day) resolves however new Date(y, m, d, h, min) normalises it; no special handling.

Acceptance criteria

  • The detail panel’s Due chip, a row’s due chip, and ⋯ → Due date → Custom… open the same picker, with the time control drawn for D1’s pick; a time commit saves and leaves it open, and a day pick still saves and closes.
  • Every due chip (row, panel, Today and By-Priority rows, select mode) reads date + time when timed — “Today 3 PM”, “Tomorrow 9:30 AM”, “Oct 12 6 PM” in en-US — and exactly as today when untimed.
  • The time survives a reload, is stored in items.due_time as a wall-clock time, and no row can hold a time without a date.
  • A timed task due today is amber before its minute and red from it, flipping within a minute without a reload; untimed bands are unchanged.
  • At that minute the task moves from its folder’s amber count to its red count and joins its parent’s overdue-subtask tally.
  • Today, a folder’s “Due date” sort and By-Priority’s tiebreak order same-day tasks by time, untimed last for the day.
  • Picking another day (grid, Today, a ⋯ preset) keeps the time; × clears only the time; clearing the date clears both; setting a time on an undated task dates it today.
  • Completing a recurring timed task spawns the next occurrence at the same time — optimistically and after a reload.
  • Converting a timed task to code (gate RPC or Classify as…), knowledge or research succeeds and clears the time with the date.
  • The classifier turns “dentist tomorrow at 3pm” into tomorrow 15:00 and “call mom tonight” into today with no time; a time without a day lands on today; it never writes a time without its date or onto a different held date.
  • A human time edit claims the row from the classifier, and dispatching a row whose guessed time was changed logs a due_time correction.
  • The API takes and returns due_time; malformed times, and a time with no (or a null) date, are rejected with 400.

Out of scope, risks & unknowns

  • Reminders need a delivery channel alfred doesn’t have (web push + a service worker, or an iOS bridge); a later story builds them on due_time plus the owner’s zone.
  • Hourly repeat stays engine-only (D2’s consequence).
  • Weekly-plan items (create_weekly_plan_items, lib/weekly-plan-items/payload.ts) stay date-only: that payload is the weekly review’s contract, and a time there is that skill’s change to ask for.
  • Risk — complete_and_spawn drift. Its column list predates priority, so the server-side spawn drops a recurring task’s priority today (a separate fix is queued). Whichever change lands second must rebuild the function from the other’s definition.
  • Risk — travel. The browser resolves “now” in the device zone, the classifier resolves “today” in CLASSIFIER_TIMEZONE; away from home they can disagree by a day near midnight. Accepted.
  • Unknown — whether Playwright’s fill() drives <input type="time"> identically in the project’s Chromium (it expects HH:MM); confirm in the first E2E.
