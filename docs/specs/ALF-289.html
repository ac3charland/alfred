ALF-289 — Reader: a “Further Reading” section for the articles a post links to

A summarised post’s overview gains a Further reading checklist of the articles it links to: tick some, then send them to the Reader for summarising or straight to Instapaper.

Decisions

  1. D1How does “Send to Reader” work?

    It saves the link into Instapaper’s “To Reader” folder; the existing leg summarises it.

    Instapaper already extracts article text. No new fetcher, parser or intake path.

  2. D2Where do the links come from?

    The post’s own links, numbered. The model picks numbers and never writes a URL.

    A URL the model writes can be invented. A number it picks can’t.

  3. D3Where does the section sit?

    Last, after “Who should read it”, with its own selection and send bar.

    Keeps the wiki bar and the reading bar apart.

  4. D4What shows when a post has none?

    Nothing: no heading and no empty line, unlike Novel ideas.

    Most essays link nothing worth reading, so an empty line on every post is noise.

  5. D5How many items at most?

    Ten, in the order the post links them.

    Roundups run long, and the other lists’ limit of six would cut them short.

  6. D6What happens to an item once sent?

    It shows “In Reader” or “In Instapaper” and can’t be ticked again.

    One send per link; moving it into To Reader in Instapaper still gets it summarised.

☞ open: your pick · → proposed: my call · ✓ settled: yours. Merging accepts every proposed row.

Not doing

No backfill: posts summarised before this ships show no Further reading unless you re-summarise them. No new fetching of web pages: every send goes through Instapaper.

Heads-up

  • Prompt goes to v2; the link list adds about a third of a cent per summary.
  • “Send to Reader” needs the “To Reader” folder in Instapaper, which already exists (ALF-272).

Working detail

Everything below is for the implementing agent. Approving needs only what’s above.

Context

The ticket. ALF-289: “Add a new section to Reader summary: ‘Further Reading’ for articles mentioned and linked to, link roundups, etc. These should be checkable like novel ideas or evidence, but they can either be sent to the reader for summarization or to Instapaper directly.” It is a story in epic ALF-232 (docs/specs/epics/ALF-232.html; build notes in docs/specs/epics/ALF-232-notes.md). The epic is background only. This spec does not change it.

What exists today.

  • The summary. One structured-output call per post (workers/src/reader/summarize.ts). The schema (schema.ts) is headline, gist and overview { novel_ideas, evidence, argument, who_should_read }. The static prompt is in prompt.ts with READER_PROMPT_VERSION = 1. The model’s input is the stored plain text, which has no URLs in it. htmlToText (workers/src/comms/email-text.ts) drops the hrefs.
  • The HTML. A newsletter’s email HTML is kept in reader_posts.html (0039, ALF-238) when it produced the text and fits READER_HTML_CHARS. An Instapaper article (0041, ALF-272) keeps no HTML: to-reader.ts strips get_text’s HTML to text and discards it. The retention sweep nulls text and html together at 90 days.
  • Links in real Substack mail. Body links are opaque substack.com/redirect/<uuid> wrappers. Chrome links (subscribe, the post itself) are substack.com/redirect/2/<base64>. There are also substack.com/@author bylines and substack.com/app-link/… links. See the fixture workers/src/reader/fixtures/essay.ts. extract.ts’s anchors() already parses anchors with a scheme filter.
  • The checklists. components/reader/wiki-picks.tsx makes Novel ideas and Evidence one selection with a “Send to wiki” bar. It mounts only where the wiki is writable. Sent marks are exact text in wiki_sent_ideas / wiki_sent_evidence, appended atomically by append_wiki_sent_picks (0040).
  • Instapaper. The app signs bookmarks/add server-side (frontend/lib/instapaper/*, the Send verb’s route app/api/reader/posts/[id]/instapaper). The Worker’s To Reader leg (workers/src/reader/to-reader.ts) lists the folder titled exactly “To Reader” each tick. It takes the oldest bookmarks into free slots under the daily ceiling, inserts them as source = 'instapaper' posts, archives them in Instapaper and summarises them. planBookmark already handles a bookmark that is already a post. Whether Instapaper is configured reaches the client as useInstapaperConfigured().

Decision records

D1How does “Send to Reader” work? · proposed

Alternatives: (a) insert a reader_posts row keyed on the URL and have the Worker fetch and extract the page itself. This needs a readability extractor, a third source, a new identity constraint and more subrequests per tick. (b) Resolve the link and hand it to the To Reader leg by some other queue. Both rebuild something Instapaper already does.

Chosen: bookmarks/add with folder_id set to the “To Reader” folder. From there the path is ALF-272’s: the leg takes the bookmark within a tick or two (subject to its free slots and the daily ceiling), archives it in Instapaper, and it appears in the list as an Instapaper post. Consequences: a sent-to-Reader item is briefly visible in Instapaper’s To Reader folder, which is where the owner puts things by hand anyway. It fails when Instapaper isn’t configured or the folder is missing, and the route says so (Behaviour). It needs no Worker change for the send.

D2Where do the links come from? · proposed

Alternatives: (a) have the model return URLs it reads from a text that carries them inline. It can mistype or invent them, and a wrong URL is a silent failure. (b) Treat every anchor as further reading without the model. That lists subscribe buttons, sponsors and bylines. (c) Store a link column at intake. That still needs the model to choose among the links.

Chosen: at summarise time a pure function numbers the post’s candidate links (D11). The model sees each link’s number inline where it sits in the text, and a numbered list with the URLs. The model returns { link: n, title, note }. Normalisation drops any number not in the list, so every stored URL is one the post contains. Titles and notes are the model’s own, because anchor text is often “this” or “here”.

D3Where does the section sit? · proposed

Alternative: directly after Evidence, beside the other checklists. That puts two selection bars in a row with different verbs (“Send to wiki” against “Send to Reader / Instapaper”), which invites sending to the wrong place. Chosen: last in the panel, after “Who should read it” and above the footer. It has its own selection state, independent of the wiki picks. Ticking a link never ticks a bullet.

D4What shows when a post has none? · proposed

Novel ideas and Evidence always render because an empty list there is a verdict (“nothing new”). An empty Further reading is not a verdict, just an essay that links nothing worth reading. So it renders nothing. The same holds for every post summarised under prompt v1, whose overview has no further_reading key.

D5How many items at most? · proposed

Alternatives: six, matching READER_MAX_BULLETS, which truncates a roundup; or no cap, which makes output tokens unbounded. Chosen: READER_MAX_FURTHER_READING = 10, enforced in normalisation (after sorting by link number), not by the grammar. schema.ts’s header explains why the grammar can’t take maxItems. Ten items at about 40 output tokens each stays well inside max_tokens 4 096.

D6What happens to an item once sent? · proposed

Alternative: allow a second send to the other destination. That makes two marks per row and one link bookmarked twice. Chosen: an item sent anywhere is out of the selection, the way a wiki-sent bullet is. The escape hatch is Instapaper’s own. Moving a bookmark into To Reader brings it into the Reader (D1’s path), and the leg’s planBookmark already handles a bookmark that is already a post.

Implementation decisions

D7 · The model input marks links inline, and the stored text is unchanged

When the post has HTML, the model input is built from it: each kept anchor’s text is followed by  [n] before htmlToText runs. This is a pre-pass on the HTML string inside workers/src/reader/links.ts, so the comms file is imported, never edited. The block --- links --- then lists [n] <url>, one per line, after the post text. The result is still capped by READER_MODEL_INPUT_CHARS (text first, then the list, truncated whole-line). Stored text and word_count are untouched. A post with no HTML (plain-text mail, pre-0039 rows, swept rows) gets --- links ---\nnone and the prompt says to return an empty list.

D8 · Stored in the overview, as URLs

Normalisation maps each kept pick to { url, title, note } and writes it as overview.further_reading in the existing jsonb. No new column holds the list, and the 90-day sweep never touches it. Items are deduped by link number and sorted by it (document order, D5).

D9 · Sent marks: two URL arrays and one atomic append

Mirrors 0040: further_sent_reader text[] and further_sent_instapaper text[], keyed by exact URL. A re-summarise that keeps a link keeps its mark, and one that drops it drops the item. An RPC appends to one of the two arrays, deduped, in first-occurrence order. Both columns go into READER_POST_LIST_COLUMNS, because they are small.

D10 · Instapaper articles keep their HTML

The To Reader leg writes get_text’s HTML into reader_posts.html, under the same READER_HTML_CHARS rule as mail, so an article’s links reach the model and Re-summarise. Guard: the article Send path (restoreOrResave) must keep re-saving by URL only. It must not start uploading this HTML as content. The column comment is updated to say the HTML can be Instapaper’s text view.

D11 · Which anchors are candidates

Deterministic filtering is kept minimal, and the model does the judging. Keep an anchor when it is http(s), has visible text, and is not one of: the post’s own canonical_url (compared on origin + path); substack.com/@…; substack.com/app-link/…; substack.com/redirect/2/… (Substack’s own chrome wrappers). Repeats of one URL share one number. The list is capped at READER_MAX_LINK_CANDIDATES = 150 in document order. Wrappers are not unwrapped or resolved in the Worker, because that would cost subrequests. bookmarks/add leaves resolve_final_url at Instapaper’s default, which follows the redirect.

D12 · The send route saves each link in turn and keeps what landed

One request per press. Links are saved one at a time with the existing 15 s per-call timeout, and the route stops starting new saves after 20 s so it answers inside the function limit. Only confirmed saves are appended. A partial send answers 200 with the row and the URLs that didn’t go. A send where nothing landed answers the first failure’s status and sentence via sendFailureResponse. The item gets no content and no tags. Its title is the model’s title and its description is the note.

D13 · Prompt v2, with no silent re-run

READER_PROMPT_VERSION becomes 2 because the instructions change. Per epic D7, nothing re-summarises existing rows. The Re-summarise verb is how an older post gains the section, and only while its HTML is still stored.

D14 · Its own component, sharing primitives with WikiPicks

components/reader/further-reading.tsx is a new checklist. Its rows differ from WikiPicks’ (two lines, plus a sibling open link that can’t nest inside the row’s button), and so does its bar (two sends). Reuse the tick styles and the row/bar class strings. If they extract cleanly into a shared reader-checklist.styles.ts without changing WikiPicks’ rendering (its Storybook baselines must not move), extract them. Otherwise import from wiki-picks.tsx’s exports rather than copying.

Behaviour

Rendering (P1)

  • The section renders only when overview.further_reading is present, well-formed and non-empty (D4). A malformed list hides the section and leaves the rest of the overview rendering. The frontend guard treats further_reading as optional.
  • Instapaper configured (useInstapaperConfigured()): the checklist in P1 “Picking”. The heading row has Select all / Deselect all over unsent items, or “All sent” with a check once none are left, as WikiPicks does. Each row is a role="checkbox" button (Space toggles, Enter does nothing) holding the title (font-medium) and note (muted) on two lines. A sibling ghost icon link ↗ (aria-label="Open <title>", target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer") opens the URL.
  • Not configured: P1 “No Instapaper”, a plain bulleted list where each title is the link and the note follows. There are no ticks and no bar. This is independent of whether the wiki is writable.
  • Sent items (P1 “After sends”): the tick slot becomes a check, and a status reads “In Reader” (the module green, MODULE_ACCENT.reader.text) or “In Instapaper” (muted). A URL in both arrays shows “In Reader”. The open link stays.
  • The bar appears when at least one item is ticked: “N selected”, Send to Reader (accent, BookOpen icon), Send to Instapaper (outline) and Clear. While a send is in flight every control in the section is disabled and the pressed button reads “Sending…” with the spinner. In-flight state lives in the store per post, as useWikiSendInFlight does.

The route intersects the requested URLs with the current overview’s list, because a re-summarise can reword things under an open tab. It drops any URL already in either sent array. The destination order is folders/list (Reader only), then the saves, then one append.

Summariser

  • The schema gains overview.further_reading: [{ link: integer, title: string, note: string }], which is required and may be empty. The guard requires it. A miss is the existing counted schema failure.
  • Prompt addition, in substance: further_reading lists the reading the post points its reader to (articles, papers, posts, videos, books) by the link numbers in the text. In a link roundup, every item is further reading. Never the publication’s own chrome (subscribe, share, comments, app, author profile, the post itself), sponsors or ads. title names the linked piece, not the anchor text. note (≤ 20 words) says what it is and why the post points to it. Up to 10. Empty when the post links nothing worth reading, or when the links block says “none”.
  • Normalisation (D2, D5, D8): drop unknown numbers, dedupe, sort by number, cap at 10, map to URLs, and trim blank titles (an item whose title is blank after trimming is dropped).
  • Subrequests: none added. A fresh post’s HTML is already in memory at intake, and a retried post’s read adds html and canonical_url to its existing select. Check this against the arithmetic in scheduled.ts’s READER_TICK_LIMIT doc comment.

Acceptance criteria

  • The summariser’s input carries [n] markers and a numbered links block built from the post’s HTML. A post without HTML gets “none”.
  • Candidates exclude non-http(s) links, links with no visible text, the canonical URL, bylines, app-links and redirect/2/ wrappers. Repeats share a number, and the list is capped at 150.
  • A stored further_reading holds only URLs from the numbered list, deduped, in link order, at most 10.
  • New summaries are stamped prompt v2, and no existing summary is re-run.
  • An Instapaper article’s HTML is stored under the HTML ceiling, and its Send still re-saves by URL only.
  • The section renders last in the overview only when it has items. A v1 post shows no section.
  • With Instapaper configured, items tick like Novel ideas. Select all ticks only unsent items, and the bar offers Send to Reader and Send to Instapaper.
  • Send to Reader saves each ticked URL into the “To Reader” folder. Send to Instapaper saves to Unread. Both mark only confirmed saves.
  • Sent items show “In Reader” (green) or “In Instapaper” (muted) and can’t be ticked.
  • A partial send marks what landed, keeps the rest ticked, and toasts “Sent k of n …”.
  • A missing To Reader folder answers 409 with its sentence, and nothing is saved.
  • Without Instapaper credentials, the section is a plain list of links.
  • Each item’s open link opens the URL in a new tab and is not nested inside the row’s checkbox button.
  • WikiPicks’ Storybook baselines don’t move, and check is green.

Out of scope, risks & unknowns

  • Backfill is out per epic D7. Re-summarise is the owner’s tool, and it works only while the post’s HTML is stored.
  • Direct page fetching is out (D1). Instapaper is the reader of the web for alfred.
  • Confirm while building: that Instapaper’s bookmarks/add with the default resolve_final_url follows a substack.com/redirect/<uuid> wrapper and that the returned bookmark’s url is the final one. The To Reader leg’s siteOf reads that URL. If Instapaper keeps the wrapper, the article’s eyebrow reads substack.com. Record the finding with the Reader’s other build notes in docs/specs/epics/ALF-232-notes.md.
  • Risk: expired wrappers. A redirect wrapper sent months later may no longer resolve, and Instapaper then saves an error page. The link is sent as it was in the post, and the likely case is a send within days of arrival.
  • Risk: the model over-picks chrome. The prompt and D11’s filter aim to prevent it. The owner judges this from the eval output, not from a test.
  • Instapaper rate limits are undocumented. Ten sequential saves per press is well below anything the send verb hasn’t already survived.
