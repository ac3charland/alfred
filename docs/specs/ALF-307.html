ALF-307 — Collapse threads in the comms FYI drawer

Opening FYI shows one row per conversation instead of one per message; open a conversation to see its messages, each still a full row with its own tier picker.

Working detail

Everything below is for the implementing agent. Approving needs only what’s above.

Context

The problem. The FYI shelf (components/comms/fyi-shelf.tsx, rendered by components/comms/comms-queue-view.tsx) draws one MessageRow per shelved message, newest first, 50 at a time. The owner reports it as "borderline unreadable" because every text in a busy chat and every reply in an email thread gets its own row. In refinement the owner named the noise sources as iMessage chats and email threads, not repeat senders. That makes the conversation, not the sender, the grouping key.

What exists. Every comm_messages row has carried thread_key since migration 0034_comms.sql: Gmail's threadId; for IMAP the root of the References chain; for iMessage the chat guid. An iMessage chat's key never changes, so grouping on it alone would merge 60 days of texting one person into one row. The owner chose to split chats on a quiet gap (D1). Rows also carry chat_name and participants. comm_accounts.kind (gmail | imap | imessage) says which rule applies.

How the shelf loads. The store holds every queued row and only a page of the shelf. readShelfPage in lib/data/comms.ts walks the newest limit shelf rows (SHELF_PAGE_SIZE = 50, growing by 50 on "Show more") and returns the shelf's total message count. Message paging can cut a conversation in half at the page edge. D7 handles that.

Epic. ALF-7 Q-"unit of the queue" anticipated this: the unit stays the message, and rolling up by thread_key "becomes a query and a view, a change to C and O, not a migration". This story applies that to the shelf only, and needs no migration. It honours D6 (the shelf stays unbadged and collapsed; its summary stays a sentence), D24/D25 (flagged rows stay visible; see D5) and D3 (the queue is untouched).

Decision records

D1What counts as one conversation? · settled

A conversation is the shelved messages that share (account_id, thread_key). For an account whose kind is imessage, that set is also split wherever two consecutive shelved messages (by received_at) are more than the gap (D2) apart. Email is never split by time, because a thread is already a bounded unit. Considered: per-thread only (an iMessage chat becomes one 60-day row), split by calendar day (cuts a conversation that runs past midnight), and grouping by sender (helps receipts and alerts, but the owner said those aren't the noise; listed under Not doing).

The gap is measured between shelved messages only. Queued and outbound rows in the same chat are not read. That keeps the rule a pure function of the rows the shelf holds, so client and server can never disagree. One consequence: a queued message in the middle of a chat burst doesn't split the burst around it.

D2How long a silence splits a chat? · proposed

CONVERSATION_GAP_MS = 6 * 60 * 60 * 1000 in the new lib/comms/conversations.ts. A gap of exactly 6 h keeps the messages together; anything longer splits them. Six hours separates an evening's exchange from the next morning's but keeps one afternoon's back-and-forth whole. Considered: 2 h (a slow conversation fragments) and 12 h (a morning and an evening chat merge). This is a constant, not a setting. Retuning it is a one-line change.

D3Order inside an opened conversation? · settled

Newest first. It matches every other list in the module, so the shelf reads in one direction all the way down. The newest message, which the collapsed row already showed, sits directly under the header, and j from the header lands on it. j/k walk the messages in the drawn order (D6).

Considered: oldest first, which reads like the chat, at the cost of reversing direction inside a newest-first shelf.

D4What a collapsed conversation shows · proposed

It keeps the row's two-line shape (Plate I), so the shelf still reads as one list:

  • Line 1, who: for an iMessage group chat (the daemon's own rule in daemon/src/sources/imessage/normalize.ts: chat_name set or participants.length > 1, where participants excludes the owner), its chat_name when it has one, else up to two participant labels and "+N". Otherwise, the distinct sender labels (senderLabel, so roster names win), newest first, up to two and then "+N". After that: the account label, the newest message's time (formatMessageTime) and "· N messages" in the same muted meta text.
  • Line 2: the newest message's askLine. FYI rows rarely carry an ask, so for email this falls through to the subject, which is the right line for a thread. When the conversation has more than one sender, prefix the newest sender's label ("Sam: …") so a group chat's line is attributable.
  • Line 3: the rolled-up chips (D5).

The count is prose in the meta line, not a Badge. The shelf is unbadged by D6 because a number in a pill reads as debt, and a dozen pills down the shelf would reintroduce exactly that. Considered: leading with the subject or chat name as a title line (adds a third line to every conversation), and a "+11" pill (a badge).

D5Can a collapsed conversation hide a warning? · proposed

No. The collapsed row shows the union of its messages' chips, each with a count when more than one message carries it ("Attachment · not read · 2"). The rollup covers Re-run pending, Priority person, Unjudged, Attachment · not read, Body · not decoded, Refused and Filtered. Priority person is never counted, since it describes a sender. Chip order matches RowMarkers.

The expiry chip ("Deleted in N days") does not roll up. In any conversation older than 53 days its oldest message always carries one, so it would sit on most old conversations and say nothing. It stays on the message rows inside, where it says which message is about to go.

Why roll up at all: D24/D25 put refused, filtered and unreadable messages on the shelf with a visible flag, and the epic is explicit that "a flag only helps someone who looks". Collapsing a refused message into a conversation that shows no sign of it would undo that.

D6How a conversation opens · proposed

A conversation is open exactly when it, or one of its messages, is the view's selected row. No separate expansion state: the existing selectedId in CommsQueueView drives it, the same way a message row's detail opens when it's selected. Clicking the conversation header toggles its selection, like a row. j/k step onto the header (opening it), then into its messages, then past it (closing it). Only one conversation is open at a time, because only one row is ever selected. The verb hotkeys (o/i/x/t) do nothing on a selected header. Verbs belong to messages. Considered: independent per-conversation open state (lets several be open, but then j/k need a second notion of "open", and opening a conversation becomes a step separate from selecting it).

Implementation decisions

D7 · The server never splits a conversation across a page

readShelfPage keeps paging by message but completes the last conversation it reached. After the walk, if the last page came back full, it reads that oldest held row's remaining shelved rows: same account_id and thread_key, received_at <= the oldest held row's (with <= plus an id dedupe, to survive ties), within the window, with the same eligibility filter. It then keeps only the rows that groupConversations places in the same conversation as that oldest held row. So every conversation the client holds is whole, its count is exact, and "Show more" only ever adds conversations below the last one.

The gap rule lives once, in TypeScript, shared by the server read and the view. Considered: a Postgres function that pages by conversation (window functions over lag(received_at)). That's a migration plus a second copy of the gap rule in SQL that must match the TS one. The repo already pays that cost once (SHELF_ELIGIBLE_FILTER ↔ isShelfEligible) and this doesn't need to. Also considered: grouping client-side over raw message pages. That's simplest, but the oldest conversation's count would be low and "Show more" would grow it in place instead of appending.

D8 · "Show more" always moves forward

Completion (D7) can return more than limit rows. showMoreShelf therefore sets the next limit to max(limit + SHELF_PAGE_SIZE, heldShelfRows + SHELF_PAGE_SIZE), still capped at SHELF_LIMIT_MAX and the shelf's last page. Without this, a page completed past the next limit would make the button a no-op. The button's label keeps counting messages (Show more (N older)), and the shelf summary keeps its message count, so neither needs a server-side conversation count.

D9 · A conversation's identity is its oldest message

id = 'conv:' + oldest.id. A new message joining the conversation doesn't change it, and loading more pages doesn't either (conversations arrive whole, D7), so an open conversation stays open through a live re-read. The prefix keeps it disjoint from message ids in orderedIds. Known edge: promoting the oldest message out of an open conversation changes the id and closes it. That's acceptable, because the owner just acted on that message and it has left the shelf.

D10 · Chips become data before they become badges

Extract RowMarkers' decision list into a pure rowMarkerKinds(message, people, now, shelved) in lib/comms/markers.ts, returning ordered kinds ('rerun-pending' | 'priority' | …). RowMarkers renders one message's kinds. The conversation header renders a rollup: rollUpMarkers(messages, …) counts kinds across messages and drops 'expiry'. A single label/variant map renders both, so a chip can't look different on a conversation and on its message.

Contracts

No schema change, no migration, no route change. The snapshot's shape is unchanged: messages just contains whole conversations. GET /api/comms/messages?scope=shelf and fetchCommMessages have no callers and are left as they are.

// lib/comms/conversations.ts (new; exported through lib/comms/index.ts)
export const CONVERSATION_GAP_MS = 6 * 60 * 60 * 1000;

export interface ShelfConversation {
  /** 'conv:' + the oldest message's id, messages.at(-1) (D9). */
  id: string;
  /** Newest → oldest, the order they are drawn in (D3). */
  messages: CommMessage[];
  /** messages[0]: what the collapsed row shows. */
  newest: CommMessage;
}

/** Group shelf rows into conversations (D1), newest conversation first. */
export function groupConversations(
  shelf: CommMessage[],
  accounts: Pick<CommAccount, 'id' | 'kind'>[],
): ShelfConversation[];

// lib/comms/markers.ts (D10)
export type RowMarkerKind =
  | 'rerun-pending' | 'priority' | 'unjudged' | 'attachment'
  | 'decode-failed' | 'expiry' | 'refused' | 'filtered';
export function rowMarkerKinds(message, people, now, shelved): RowMarkerKind[];
export function rollUpMarkers(messages, people, now): { kind: RowMarkerKind; count: number }[];

Acceptance criteria

  • Opening FYI shows one row per conversation: shelved messages sharing account + thread_key collapse into one row.
  • iMessage shelved messages more than 6 h apart in the same chat are separate conversations; exactly 6 h apart stays one. Email is never split by time.
  • A one-message conversation renders exactly as today's shelf row.
  • A collapsed conversation shows who (chat name or up to two names + "+N"), account, newest time, "· N messages" as meta text (not a badge), and the newest message's line, prefixed "Sender:" when there are several senders.
  • A collapsed conversation shows each chip any of its messages carries, with a count when more than one does. Priority person is uncounted; the expiry chip doesn't roll up.
  • An opened conversation lists its messages as full shelf rows (tier picker included), indented, newest first.
  • Selecting a conversation opens it. j/k walk onto it, through its messages and past it, and only one is open at a time. Esc closes it. Verb hotkeys do nothing on its header.
  • A conversation is never cut by a page edge (its count is exact on first load), and "Show more" always appends at least one new conversation while older ones remain.
  • Promoting a message out of a conversation removes just that message; at one remaining message the conversation renders as a plain row.
  • The shelf summary, its unbadged collapsed state and its lazy mount are unchanged.

Out of scope, risks & unknowns

  • Repeat senders (out). The owner said receipts and alerts aren't the noise. If they become it, sender grouping is a second key over these conversations, not a replacement.
  • Queue tiers (out). The unit of the queue is ALF-7's largest open question, tangled with reply detection and tier conflicts. This story doesn't answer it by accident.
  • Whole-conversation verbs (out). Re-tiering a whole conversation would write N corrections from one gesture into the example set (D11), which deserves its own decision.
  • Risk: one huge burst. A 300-text group-chat evening is one conversation and one completion read. It's bounded by the shelf's own size and paged by readAllPages, so it costs more but stays correct.
