ALF-370 — Make habit entry more mobile-friendly

On a phone, the history grid’s day squares grow to thumb size, and tapping one opens the day editor as a bottom sheet with full-size controls. Desktop is unchanged.

Decisions

  1. D2Which devices get the touch treatment?

    Any screen under 768px wide, or any touch-first device at any width.

    Width alone hands the small controls back when the phone is turned sideways.

  2. D3How big are the day squares?

    38px squares on a 44px pitch, the whole pitch tappable.

    44px is the standard thumb target. Costs: about seven weeks on screen, not nine.

  3. D4How is a yes/no criterion recorded?

    Two buttons, Yes and No; tapping the lit one again clears it.

    Cycling through ✓ to reach ✕ lets one slip record the wrong answer.

  4. D5How does the sheet close?

    A full-width Done button, a tap outside, or Escape. No swipe.

    Nothing needs saving; Done is a thumb-sized exit. Swipe needs a gesture library.

  5. D1Where does the editor open on a phone?

    A bottom sheet, opened from the grid; the grid stays the only way in.

☞ open: your pick · → proposed: my call · ✓ settled: yours. Merging accepts every proposed row.

Not doing

No Today shortcut outside the grid. No change to creating or editing a habit on a phone. No swipe-to-dismiss.

Working detail

Everything below is for the implementing agent. Approving needs only what’s above.

Context

ALF-370 belongs to epic ALF-146 (docs/specs/epics/ALF-146.html). The ticket carried a title only, so scope was pinned with the owner in the refinement session:

  • Scope: logging a day only. The habit create/edit form is untouched.
  • What hurts on a phone: the day squares are too small to hit, and the controls inside the editor are too small. The popover’s positioning and the number of steps were not named as problems.
  • Direction: keep the grid as the only entry point; on a phone the editor opens as a bottom sheet with 44px controls. An always-visible Today row was offered and declined.

What exists today

  • frontend/components/habits/history-grid.tsx renders the grid and hosts one Radix popover for the whole grid, positioned on the open cell through a virtual anchor. Focus returns to the cell on close.
  • history-cell.tsx draws a 26px square (h-[26px] w-[26px]); the grid’s row template, the weekday labels’ line-height and CELL_SIZE_PX in habits.styles.ts all repeat that 26. Squares sit 6px apart, so the tappable area is 26×26 with dead gaps between.
  • day-editor.tsx is a fixed 265px column: a 13px date, a verdict badge, one row per criterion, a cost line and a 20px ⋯ button. A yes/no criterion is a 17px box that cycles unrecorded → ✓ → ✕ → unrecorded. A measured criterion is a 92px field that commits on blur or Enter. Writes are debounced 250ms and flushed on unmount. skip-panel.tsx replaces the editor in place for the skip step.
  • Phone conventions already in the repo: MOBILE_QUERY and useMediaQuery (lib/hooks/use-media-query.ts); useVisualViewport and the full-screen SheetDialog that rides above the on-screen keyboard (components/atoms/dialog.tsx); a global rule lifting every input to 16px on coarse pointers (app/globals.css). There is no bottom-anchored sheet yet.

Epic §6 settles that tapping a day opens a derived-only editor and that skipped lives in an overflow menu. Neither changes here. The epic does not say the editor is a popover; that came from the day-editor story, and D1 departs from it on touch devices only.

Decision records

D1Where does the editor open on a phone? · settled

The owner’s pick. Considered: an always-visible Today row on each card plus a sheet for past days (declined: the grid stays the one way in); keeping the popover and only enlarging targets (declined: least improvement). Consequence: finding today still means finding its square, so D3’s larger squares and the existing teal ring carry that on their own.

D2Which devices get the touch treatment? · proposed

One switch, TOUCH_QUERY, true when the viewport is under 48rem wide or the primary pointer is coarse. It drives all three changes together: square size, sheet versus popover, and control size.

Considered: width only, the repo’s MOBILE_QUERY — a phone in landscape is 844px wide, so it would fall back to 26px squares and the small popover on the very device the ticket is about. Coarse pointer only — a narrow desktop window would keep a popover that has little room. Two independent switches (size by pointer, container by width) — four combinations to test for no gain.

Consequences: a tablet gets the sheet too, so the sheet is capped at 28rem wide and centred. A narrow desktop window with a mouse gets the touch treatment; harmless.

D3How big are the day squares? · proposed

38px squares, the 6px gap unchanged, giving a 44px pitch. Each cell’s hit area extends 3px past its square on every side, so neighbouring targets meet and no tap lands in a dead gap.

Considered: 34px on a 40px pitch — nine weeks become about seven and a half instead of seven, but the target stays under the 44px that Apple’s guidelines and WCAG 2.5.5 name. Keeping 26px and only widening the hit area — the most it can reach without overlapping is 32px. Consequence: at 390px wide about seven weeks are visible instead of nine, and each card grows about 85px taller. Older weeks are still one sideways scroll away.

D4How is a yes/no criterion recorded? · proposed

In touch mode only, the tri-state box becomes two buttons. Considered: the same cycling box at 44px — the size was half the problem; the other half is that reaching ✕ means passing through ✓, so a slip or a double-tap records the opposite answer. Two buttons make either answer one deliberate tap. The third state survives because the model needs it: an unrecorded criterion and one recorded as “no” are different days.

Desktop keeps the cycling box, where a precise pointer makes it fast and its tests and muscle memory already exist.

D5How does the sheet close? · proposed

The editor has no Save: every change commits itself. A sheet with no visible way out reads as unfinished on a phone, and a small × in the top corner is the furthest point from the thumb. Done only closes. Considered: swipe-down — it needs a gesture library or hand-rolled touch handling for something a tap outside already does; no drag handle is drawn, because a handle promises a drag.

Implementation decisions

D6 · Squares resize in CSS; the container and controls switch in JS

The grid is server-rendered, and useMediaQuery answers false until hydration, so sizing squares from the hook would paint 26px and jump to 38px. Square geometry is therefore CSS behind a touch: variant. The editor only mounts after a tap, so the hook is safe there: HistoryGrid reads it once and passes the result to both the container choice and DayEditor, so the two cannot disagree.

D7 · A new bottom-sheet atom that follows the visible viewport

iOS Safari does not shrink the layout viewport for the on-screen keyboard, so a plain fixed bottom-0 sheet would sit underneath it the moment the skip reason or a count is focused. The atom positions its bottom edge from useVisualViewport(), as SheetDialog already sizes itself, and falls back to bottom-0 where the API is absent. It lives beside the other dialog scaffolds in components/atoms/dialog.tsx.

D8 · One editor, one touch prop

DayEditor and SkipPanel gain a touch flag rather than a phone-only copy. The commit, debounce, flush and verdict logic stays in one place; only sizing and the yes/no control branch.

D9 · A typed value is saved however the editor closes

A measured field commits on blur or Enter. On a touch device neither is guaranteed before the editor goes away: tapping a button does not reliably blur a field in iOS Safari, and a removed element does not fire blur. So a field holding an uncommitted draft commits it when the editor unmounts, in both modes. This extends the principle the editor already states for its debounce: closing is not a reason to lose what was just recorded.

D10 · Skip stays behind ⋯

Epic §6 puts skipped in an overflow menu so it is not a fourth thing to tap past each morning. The sheet keeps that; the ⋯ button and its menu item only grow to 44px.

Behaviour

Touch mode

Touch mode is on when TOUCH_QUERY matches (D2). Everything below applies only in touch mode. With it off, the grid, the popover and the editor render exactly as they do today.

  • The corner radius, plate colours, today ring, legend, right-edge anchoring and sideways scroll are unchanged.
  • A day that cannot be logged stays inert and out of the tab order; its square grows, but it has no hit area.
  • The sizes come from CSS alone, so the first paint is already the right size (D6).

The sheet (P1 · Sheet states)

  • Tapping a loggable square opens a modal bottom sheet instead of the popover: a dimmed, blurred overlay (the shared DialogOverlay), focus trapped inside, page scroll locked.
  • It spans the full width up to 28rem, centred when the screen is wider. Top corners are rounded 16px; the surface, border and glow match the app’s dialogs. Bottom padding adds env(safe-area-inset-bottom).
  • Its bottom edge sits at the bottom of the visible viewport, so it rises with the on-screen keyboard (D7).
  • It is never taller than the visible viewport less 16px. When the criteria do not fit, the criterion rows scroll; the header, cost line and Done stay in place.
  • The header shows the date and, beneath it, the habit’s name. The popover was anchored to its square, so the card said which habit it was; the sheet covers the card, so it has to say so itself. The sheet’s accessible name is “{date} — {habit name}”.
  • On opening, focus goes to the sheet itself, not to a field, so no keyboard or picker appears uninvited. The skip step still focuses its reason field, as it does today.
  • Done, a tap on the overlay, and Escape all close it (D5). Focus returns to the square it was opened from.
  • If touch mode flips while the editor is open (a desktop window resized across 768px), the editor stays open on the same day in the other container, and nothing typed is lost.

Yes/no (D4)

  • A group named by the criterion’s label, holding two buttons named “Yes” and “No”, each with aria-pressed.
  • Tapping an unlit button records that answer. Tapping the lit button clears the criterion back to unrecorded, removing its key from the results as the cycling box does today.
  • Yes lit is green, No lit is red, an unlit half is the neutral border with muted text. The glyphs ✓ and ✕ sit beside the words, so the answer never rests on colour alone.
  • Each tap goes through the editor’s existing commit path: the verdict badge and cost line re-derive at once, and the write is debounced 250ms.

Measured fields

  • Commit on blur or Enter, as today, and also when the editor unmounts with an uncommitted draft (D9). An untouched field still commits nothing, so opening and closing an unlogged day does not log it.
  • Clearing a field removes the criterion’s key, as today.

The skip step and a skipped day

  • The skip step keeps its wording and its rule that a reason must be typed. The reason field is full width, 44px tall, 16px. Cancel and “Skip this day” are 44px tall and share the width equally.
  • Skipping closes the sheet, as it closes the popover today. Cancel returns to the criteria.
  • A skipped day shows its reason at 15px in place of the criterion rows, then the cost line, ⋯ and Done.

Contracts

No route, schema or store change. The component surface, as a sketch (names are the implementer’s to refine):

Plan

Read first: the frontend-architecture, react, tailwindcss, shadcn-ui, react-testing-library, playwright and storybook skills. Red/green throughout.

  1. The switch. Add TOUCH_QUERY and the touch: custom variant.
  2. The grid. In habits.styles.ts, declare the touch geometry beside CELL_SIZE_PX and move the square, row-template, label and connector sizing into exported class strings carrying both sizes (h-[26px] touch:h-[38px] …), unit-tested in habits.styles.test.ts like the plates. Apply them in history-cell.tsx and history-grid.tsx. Add the tiled hit area to the cell’s button.
  3. The atom. Add BottomSheetDialog to components/atoms/dialog.tsx: Root → Portal → DialogOverlay → Content, bottom-anchored through useVisualViewport(), capped width and height, a flex column so a caller’s middle region can scroll. Unit-test it in dialog.test.tsx.
  4. The yes/no control. Check ToggleButton, OptionButton and CheckboxButton in components/atoms/ before adding anything; add an atom only if none can carry a pressed, toned 44px button. No raw <button> in the feature component.
  5. The editor. Add touch and habitName to DayEditor; branch the sizing, the yes/no control and the Done button. Add touch to SkipPanel. Implement D9.

    D9 has an ordering trap. On unmount React runs a parent’s effect cleanup before its children’s, so DayEditor’s existing flush has already run when a MeasuredField’s cleanup fires; a child that only calls onCommit then would queue a write nobody sends. Have the editor’s own flush collect the fields’ uncommitted drafts, or give the late commit a path that writes immediately.

  6. The host. In HistoryGrid, read useMediaQuery(TOUCH_QUERY). Keep one openDate state and one editor element; open the popover when touch is off and the sheet when it is on. Reuse the existing focus-return handler for both. Done calls the same close as Escape.
  7. Stories, e2e, demo. See Tests and Demo evidence.

Acceptance criteria

  • With touch mode off (a wide viewport and a fine pointer), the grid, popover and editor are unchanged: the existing habit unit tests, e2e specs and Storybook baselines pass without edits to their desktop expectations.
  • Touch mode is on at 390px wide with a fine pointer, and at 1024px wide with a coarse pointer.
  • In touch mode each square is 38 × 38px on a 44px pitch, and a tap anywhere in a loggable day’s 44 × 44px area opens that day, including a tap in the gap beside its square.
  • In touch mode the newest week is still against the right edge on arrival and older weeks scroll into view; connectors still join linked days across the 6px gap and the Sunday–Monday stubs still show.
  • In touch mode tapping a loggable square opens a bottom sheet, not a popover, named “{date} — {habit name}”, showing the date, the habit’s name and the derived verdict.
  • In the sheet every control and button is at least 44px tall: time and number fields, Yes, No, ⋯, its menu item, Done, the skip reason field, Cancel and “Skip this day”.
  • In touch mode a yes/no criterion offers Yes and No. Tapping one records it and lights it; tapping the lit one clears the criterion; the verdict badge and cost line follow each tap.
  • Done, a tap on the overlay and Escape each close the sheet, save what was recorded, repaint the square, and return focus to it.
  • ⋯ → “Mark as skipped…” opens the skip step inside the sheet; a typed reason enables “Skip this day”, which skips the day and closes the sheet; Cancel returns to the criteria.
  • Opening a skipped day shows its reason in place of the criterion rows.
  • The first paint of /habits on a phone already has 38px squares: no resize after hydration.

Review focus

  • A count is typed and Done is tapped while the field still has focus and has never blurred → the value is saved and the square repaints.
  • The on-screen keyboard opens for a count or the skip reason → the focused field and the sheet’s buttons stay visible above it.
  • A habit with eight criteria on a 390 × 600px viewport → the rows scroll inside the sheet; the header, cost line and Done stay reachable.
  • Yes then No tapped within 250ms → one write, recording No.
  • A save fails while the sheet is open → the store’s rollback and toast behave as today, and the toast is visible above the overlay without trapping Done.

Out of scope, risks & unknowns

  • Today shortcut. Offered and declined in refinement (D1). If today’s square is still slow to find at 38px, that is the follow-up to reopen.
  • Habit create/edit on a phone. The sentence form and its criterion popovers have the same small-control problem. Scoped out by the owner; worth its own ticket.
  • Swipe-to-dismiss, and stepping to the previous or next day inside the sheet. Neither was asked for; the first needs a gesture dependency.
  • Unknown: the or media query. Confirm Tailwind’s compiler passes (width < 48rem) or (pointer: coarse) through @custom-variant unchanged. If not, the comma form (width < 48rem), (pointer: coarse) means the same and works in matchMedia too.
  • Unknown: clearing a time on iOS. A recorded time is cleared on desktop by emptying the field. Check on a real iPhone that the native time picker offers a way to empty it. If it does not, a recorded time cannot be un-recorded on a phone; that predates this story, so raise it with the owner rather than inventing a control nobody approved.
  • Risk: toast over the sheet. The toast viewport is z-[60] at the bottom right, above the dialog layer. A failure toast will overlap the sheet’s lower corner while it shows. Acceptable if Done stays tappable once it clears; do not restack the toast in this story.
  • Risk: taller cards. Each card is about 85px taller in touch mode (D3). With several habits the page is noticeably longer.
