ALF-150 — Habit tracker: the stats rail

The six figures beside each habit's history grid, plus the formation badge and its meter — and the seed plumbing that makes the all-history figures true rather than window-shaped.

Read the epic first. docs/specs/epics/ALF-146.html carries the settled decisions this story implements — D13 (the six figures are a first-class part of the view), D6 (the formation ladder), §6 (stats: side rail), and §3.1 (what each figure means). Nothing here re-takes those decisions; the S-decisions below are the ones this refinement had to make on top of them.

1 · What this story is

Slice 9 of the habit-tracker epic. Everything it needs already exists and is merged:

  • lib/habits — computeHabitStats() already returns all six figures plus the formation stage (slice 3), and already takes an optional window that scopes the hit rate and the counts without touching the all-history scalars.
  • HabitsProvider — the optimistic store seeded by the shell, holding the habit definitions, a trailing window of entries and the owner's local today (slice 7).
  • HabitCard — renders the name, the cadence summary and the history grid, inside a flex row whose comment says in as many words that it is laid out for the rail's arrival (slice 8).

So the visible half of this story is small: one presentational component in the gap the card already leaves. The other half is the reason the story isn't trivial — three of the six figures are all-history figures, and the client only holds 120 days. Handed that window, computeHabitStats reads every day before it as unlogged, so a habit older than the window would show a truncated longest streak and a demoted formation stage. Shipping the rail without fixing that would ship a number that quietly becomes wrong on day 121 — which is precisely the figure ("how much have I banked") the epic added the stage to protect.

Out of the box, in one line

Log a day and the streak beside the grid moves in the same frame; reload and the server agrees with what the tap already showed.

2 · Decisions locked during this refinement

owner marks the two the owner chose in the refinement session; the rest follow from those plus the epic and existing repo convention.

S1The all-history figures come from a server-seeded baselineowner

The shell seeds, alongside the windowed entries, a per-habit HabitStats computed on the server over all history. The store holds it untouched as a baseline; the rail shows it at rest and nudges it as the owner edits days in-session.

Why: this is what epic §4.3 pencils in ("the read endpoint returns them as precomputed scalars alongside the windowed entries, and the optimistic store adjusts them locally on a write rather than recomputing from the window"). The two alternatives were put to the owner and declined: widening the seed to all history reverses the epic's bounded-window decision and grows without bound, and computing from the window only ships a stage that silently demotes the habit after four months — the one thing D6 exists to prevent.

S2Hit rate and misses-left cover the grid's window, not all historyowner

The two window figures are computed over exactly the span the grid draws, so a reviewer can count the squares and arrive at the rail's percentage. Both figures name their span in their title / accessible description ("hit rate over the last 120 days").

Why: epic §6.2 states the property plainly — "every number in the rail is derived from the grid beside it, so the two can be checked against each other." A lifetime hit rate is more stable but unfalsifiable from the screen. allowanceRemaining is a trailing-7-day figure and is therefore inside any window at all; it is a "window figure" only in the sense that the local walk owns it.

S3Two walks and a splice — whichever walk knows more wins the streak

The rail runs computeHabitStats twice over the seeded window: once over the entries as seeded (atSeed) and once over the entries as they are now (live). A pure statsWithBaseline(baseline, atSeed, live) then splices the three (§4.2). The current streak takes max(live, baseline + drift).

Why two walks and not a delta on write: a delta can express "+1 met day" but not "you just corrected a day in the middle of the run and broke it". Re-walking the window is the same code path the grid already runs, so the rail and the connectors beside it can never disagree.
Why max for the current streak: the two estimates are wrong in opposite, non-overlapping directions. The live walk under-reports only when the run is longer than the window; the baseline under-reports only when the server's UTC "today" is a day ahead of the owner's (the store already corrects today on mount for exactly this reason — see §4.4), in which case the server counts the owner's unlogged current day as spent and the local walk doesn't. Taking the larger picks the walk that saw more, in both cases.

S4The average streak is the baseline's, unadjusted in-session

averageStreak is taken from the baseline whenever there is one, falling back to the live walk otherwise. It refreshes on the next load rather than moving with an edit.

Why: it is a mean of ended runs over all history — deltas don't compose on a mean, and reconstructing one would need the run lengths the baseline deliberately doesn't carry. It is also the figure least sensitive to a single day: the current run is excluded from it by definition (§3.1), so logging today can only change it by ending a run, and the number it would move to is visible one reload later. The fallback matters for a habit created in-session, whose whole life is inside the window.

S5Six figures, six labels — the pairing is visual only

Each figure is its own element with its own visible label and its own data-figure hook. Two figures share a line to keep the card one band tall, separated by an aria-hidden "·" — the epic's compact "33 · 14 / longest · average" rendered as two labelled columns rather than one string.

Why: read aloud, "33 · 14, longest · average" is not a sentence, and the rail is the one place in the view where the answer to "how is this going?" lives. Two columns keep the picture (§5) and give a screen reader "33, longest. 14, average." The data-figure attributes mirror the grid's data-date / data-status convention, so the E2E can address a figure without matching on a number that changes with the calendar.

S6The hedge is in the words the owner reads, not only in the docs

Stage labels are Fully Deliberate, Gaining Momentum, Nearing Automaticity, Possibly Established, and the meter's caption reads 47 of ~66 banked days — tilde included. Past the marker it reads 82 banked days · past ~66.

Why: D6 requires it — 66 is the median of an 18–254 day range, so the ladder "must be labelled as approximate in the UI". These four strings are new; nothing in the app renders a stage today (the API returns the raw possibly_established enum, which stays as it is).

S7One seeder, one read — the window read folds into the history read

getHabits() + getHabitEntries() become a single getHabitSeed() that reads every habit and its whole entry history once, computes the baseline stats, and hands the client only the windowed slice of entries.

Why: the baseline needs all history, so keeping a separate 120-day entry read would issue a second query for rows the first already returned. One read is strictly less work than the shell does today, and it keeps the window in one place instead of two. The cost is that the shell now pages the full entry history on every load — accepted at single-user scale, with the escape hatch already named in lib/data/habits.ts ("if entry volume ever makes this expensive the answer is a SQL rollup of those scalars, not a truncated read").

S8A seed failure degrades to the window walk, never to a blank shell

If the entry read fails, getHabitSeed() falls back to the existing getHabits() and returns no entries and no baselines. The rail then renders the live walk verbatim — right for every habit younger than the window, understated for older ones, and never absent.

Why: the existing seeders already swallow their error ("a seed failure must not blank the whole shell"), and the same reasoning applies harder to a derived rail: a habit card with no numbers beside it is a better failure than a habit view that doesn't render. The "no baseline" path is not a rare branch, either — every habit created in-session takes it.

4.2 statsWithBaseline

// frontend/lib/habits/baseline.ts

/**
 * The rail's figures for a client that holds a trailing window of entries plus the server's
 * all-history baseline.
 *
 * `atSeed` is the window walked over the entries AS SEEDED; `live` is the same window walked
 * over the entries as they are now. At rest the two are identical, so the rail shows the
 * server's numbers exactly; every in-session edit moves the rail by the amount it moved the
 * window walk. Without a baseline the live walk IS the whole truth — a habit created this
 * session has no history outside the window.
 */
export function statsWithBaseline(
  baseline: HabitStats | undefined,
  atSeed: HabitStats,
  live: HabitStats,
): HabitStats {
  if (baseline === undefined) return live;

  // Whichever walk saw more: `live` is short only when the run outruns the window, the
  // baseline only when the server's UTC today ran ahead of the owner's (§4.4).
  const currentStreak = Math.max(
    0,
    live.currentStreak,
    baseline.currentStreak + (live.currentStreak - atSeed.currentStreak),
  );
  const metDaysTotal = Math.max(0, baseline.metDaysTotal + (live.counts.met - atSeed.counts.met));

  return {
    currentStreak,
    longestStreak: Math.max(baseline.longestStreak, live.longestStreak, currentStreak),
    averageStreak: baseline.averageStreak ?? live.averageStreak,
    allowanceRemaining: live.allowanceRemaining,
    hitRate: live.hitRate,
    metDaysTotal,
    stage: formationStage(metDaysTotal),
    counts: live.counts,
  };
}

4.3 The path, end to end

One read on the server; two walks and a splice on the client. The grid and the rail run the same walk over the same entries, so a link the grid draws and the streak the rail counts are the same fact rendered twice.

4.4 The two "today"s, and why max is load-bearing

The store already carries a wrinkle this story inherits: the shell renders on the server, which cannot know the browser's zone, so today is seeded as the UTC date and corrected in a mount effect. The baseline is computed with the server's UTC today; both client walks use the corrected local today.

That matters in one window of the day. An owner in America/New_York at 21:00 on the 29th is living the 29th; UTC has already rolled to the 30th. The server's walk therefore treats the 29th as a past, unlogged day — a spent day — and can report a broken streak for a habit the owner simply hasn't logged yet this evening. The local walk exempts today correctly and reports the run alive. Taking the larger of the two is what keeps that evening from showing a false zero, and it costs nothing in the ordinary case where the walks agree.

Don't "fix" this by passing the browser's zone into the seed. The server render has no access to it — that is the whole reason the store corrects today in an effect — and rendering a zone-resolved date server-side is a hydration mismatch. The splice is the fix.

Tokens only Existing named accents: text-accent-green for a live streak, text-muted-foreground for a zero one and every label, bg-accent-teal for the meter fill and the stage badge (via Badge variant="accent"), border-border for the rules. No new colour token, and nothing hard-coded.
Numerals tabular-nums on every figure, so a streak ticking 9 → 10 doesn't shift the label under it.
Group The rail is a role="group" named "<habit> stats", matching the grid's "<habit> history", so a screen-reader user can tell two stacked habits apart.

Motion No transition on the figures. A number that animates while the owner is reading it is noise, and the tap already repaints the cell beside it.

6 · The seed

// frontend/lib/data/habits.ts

/**
 * The shell's habit seed: every habit, the trailing {@link APP_WINDOW_DAYS} of entries, and the
 * all-history stats the rail's banked / longest / average figures need.
 *
 * ONE read backs all three (S7): the baseline needs full history, so windowing the entry read as
 * well would re-fetch rows this read already returned. The window is applied in TS afterwards.
 */
export async function getHabitSeed(): Promise<{
  habits: Habit[];
  entries: HabitEntry[];
  stats: Record<string, HabitStats>;
}> {
  const supabase = await createClient();
  const today = todayIn('UTC');
  const { habits, entriesByHabit, error } = await getHabitsWithHistory(supabase, {
    includeArchived: false,
  });

  // Degrade in layers, never to a blank shell (S8): without entries the rail falls back to the
  // live window walk, which is right for every habit younger than the window.
  if (error) return { habits: await getHabits(), entries: [], stats: {} };

  const from = appWindow(today).from;
  const entries: HabitEntry[] = [];
  const stats: Record<string, HabitStats> = {};
  for (const habit of habits) {
    const all = entriesByHabit.get(habit.id) ?? [];
    stats[habit.id] = computeHabitStats(habit, all, today);   // no window ⇒ all history
    entries.push(...all.filter((entry) => entry.entry_date >= from));
  }
  return { habits, entries, stats };
}
  • Display order is preserved. getHabitsWithHistory orders by archived_at, sort_order, created_at; with archived habits excluded that is the order getHabits() produces today. Pin it with a test rather than an assumption.
  • The window bound is appWindow(today).from, i.e. today − 119, computed against the server's UTC today. The old seeder deliberately reached one day further back so a browser west of UTC still has its local today; keep that generosity by leaving getHabitSeed's filter at today − APP_WINDOW_DAYS and letting the client walk the narrower appWindow(localToday) inside it. The invariant to hold: the client's window is always a subset of the seeded entries, in either direction of the date line.
  • getHabitEntries() goes away along with its tests; getHabits() stays, now with two callers (the seed's fallback path and its own tests).
  • The layout drops two entries from its Promise.all and adds one, then passes initialStats={seed.stats} to HabitsProvider.

7 · Store additions

export interface HabitsState {
  habits: Habit[];
  entries: Record<string, Record<string, HabitEntry>>;
  today: string;
  /** The entries exactly as seeded — the reference the rail measures in-session edits against. */
  seedEntries: Record<string, Record<string, HabitEntry>>;
  /** Server-computed all-history stats per habit id. Absent for a habit created this session. */
  baselineStats: Record<string, HabitStats>;
}
  • No action touches either new field. They are seed-time constants that live in state because the selector needs them; the reducer's existing cases pass them through untouched, and assertNever keeps the switch exhaustive as before.
  • The selector follows useUnloggedTodayCount: read state, derive in a React.useMemo, return a plain value. 
    export function useHabitStats(habit: Habit): HabitStats {
  const state = useStateValue('useHabitStats');
  return React.useMemo(() => {
    const window = appWindow(state.today);
    const walk = (rows: Record<string, HabitEntry> | undefined) =>
      computeHabitStats(habit, Object.values(rows ?? {}), state.today, window);
    return statsWithBaseline(
      state.baselineStats[habit.id],
      walk(state.seedEntries[habit.id]),
      walk(state.entries[habit.id]),
    );
  }, [habit, state]);
}
  • Cost: two extra 120-day walks per habit per state change, memoized. At the epic's expected volume (a handful of habits) that is far below the grid's own per-cell rendering — but the memo's dependency on the whole state object means every store change re-walks every habit. Acceptable, and the same tradeoff useUnloggedTodayCount already makes.
  • test-utils gains habits.stats (default {}), so a test that doesn't care about the baseline gets the live-walk path — the same path a freshly-created habit takes.

If the owner would rather it disappeared, that is a one-line change and the mockup in §5.2 is the one to re-sign-off.
  2. Q2 — Should the hit rate's span be visible rather than only in the title? "94% · hit rate" reads clean; "94% · hit rate, 120d" is honest but noisier. Deferred until the rail has been lived with — the answer depends on whether the owner ever wonders what it covers.
  3. Q3 — The one corner the splice gets wrong. Breaking a run longer than the window during a session over-reports the current streak until the next load (§4.2, last row). Correcting it would need either unbounded entries in the client (rejected — it's the epic's window decision) or a re-fetch on write (rejected — the app deliberately reads only through the shell seed, per ALF-147 S2). Flagged, not fixed; the number self-heals on reload and the situation requires a 120-day run plus a same-session correction.

11 · Acceptance criteria

The figures

  • Every habit card shows six figures beside its grid — current streak, longest, average, hit rate, misses left, and the formation stage with its banked-days meter.
  • Each figure has its own visible label and its own data-figure attribute; no figure's meaning depends on a neighbouring one.
  • Null renders as an em dash, never as zero, for the average streak and the hit rate.
  • The stage label is hedged ("Possibly Established") and the meter caption carries the ~66 tilde.
  • The hit rate and misses-left name their span in a title.

Correctness

  • At rest, the rail shows the server's all-history figures exactly — a habit with 200 days of history shows its true longest streak and banked-day count, not a window-truncated one.
  • Logging or correcting a day moves the rail in the same frame as the cell, with no refetch and no router.refresh().
  • A failed write rolls the figures back along with the entry.
  • A habit created in-session renders a correct rail with no baseline at all.
  • The hit rate reconciles with the grid: it covers exactly the window the grid draws.
  • An evening in a zone behind UTC does not show a false zero current streak (§4.4).
  • A seed failure renders the view with live-walk figures rather than blanking it.

Layout & accessibility

  • The rail sits beside the grid on a wide screen and wraps beneath it on a narrow one, with the divider rule following (§5.1, §5.3).
  • The grid keeps its own horizontal scroll — a long history never pushes the rail off-screen or squeezes it.
  • The rail is a role="group" named for its habit, and the meter carries a spelled-out accessible name.
  • Figures use tabular numerals and existing colour tokens only.

Plumbing

  • The shell issues one habit-entry read, not two, and seeds the baseline alongside the windowed entries.
  • APP_WINDOW_DAYS is the single definition of the app's window — the grid, the seed and the rail all read it.
  • The client's stats window is always a subset of the seeded entries, whichever side of UTC the browser sits on.

Evidence

  • npm run check is green, with no new lint/format suppressions and no config weakened.
  • Every rule in §4.2 has a test in baseline.test.ts; the three Storybook baselines are committed.
  • The E2E asserts the rail's current streak before and after a reload.
  • A demo doc under docs/demos/ reproduces via npm run demo -- verify and shows the states listed in §9.
