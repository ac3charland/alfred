alfred Reader · Refinement spec

ALF-344 — Import articles from RSS and Atom feeds

Paste a blog's feed URL (e.g. https://mlarson.org/feed.xml) on the publications page. Each new post then lands in the reading list, summarised like a newsletter.

Decisions

  1. D5Break the epic's "no RSS" non-goal?

    Yes. Feeds become a fourth post source, sharing the 30-a-day summary ceiling.

    You asked for it. Instapaper already broke "one source", and the epic shaped the tables for exactly this.

  2. D6Where does a broken feed show?

    On its own card: an amber line saying what failed and since when.

    A per-feed fault needs a per-feed fix. A header note would nag on every Reader view.

  3. D7Check the URL when it's added?

    Yes. Fetch it once, refuse anything that isn't RSS or Atom, and name the card from the feed's title.

    Pasting a homepage is the likeliest mistake. It should fail at the field, not silently an hour later.

  4. D1Where are feeds added?

    On the publications roster: a feed is a card with the same toggle and summary type.

  5. D2What does a new feed backfill?

    Entries published in the last seven days.

  6. D3What about excerpt-only feeds?

    Fetch the entry's page and read the article from it.

  7. D4How often is a feed polled?

    Hourly, as a leg of the existing five-minute Reader tick.

☞ open: your pick · → proposed: my call · ✓ settled: yours. Merging accepts every proposed row.

Not doing

Finding the feed from a homepage URL, removing a feed (pause it), merging a post that arrives both by email and by feed, or re-summarising an entry that's edited after import.

Heads-up

  • Feed posts count toward the 30-a-day summary ceiling. A prolific feed can crowd out newsletters on a busy day.
  • The build session's network must allow mlarson.org to capture the real-feed fixture (§ unknowns).

Working detail

Everything below is for the implementing agent. Approving needs only what’s above.

Context

The ticket asks for articles imported via feed.xml URLs, with https://mlarson.org/feed.xml as the motivating case. The owner refined the scope in this session: feeds go on the publications roster (D1), the first poll takes the last seven days (D2), excerpt-only feeds fall back to the entry's page (D3), and each feed is polled hourly inside the existing tick (D4).

What exists. The Reader (epic ALF-232, docs/specs/epics/ALF-232.html) has three post sources on reader_posts.source:

  • gmail: newsletters from the comms mirror, matched to reader_publications by sender handle (0035).
  • instapaper: the To Reader leg (ALF-272, 0041).
  • research: reports from the research Routine (0047).

A publication carries an enable toggle and a summary_kind (ALF-322, 0052). The tick lives in workers/src/reader/scheduled.ts. Its order is config → ceiling → health → discovery → roster → worklist → To Reader → loop, and it is bound by READER_TICK_LIMIT = 6 posts and the 50-subrequest budget, whose table lives in that constant's doc comment. to-reader.ts is the template for a non-mail leg: pure planning functions, then an intake that reads → INSERTs (the claim) → summarises. comms/email-text.ts exports htmlToText and truncateAtCodePointBoundary. extract.ts owns READER_TEXT_CHARS / READER_HTML_CHARS.

Where sources disagree. Epic §5 lists "No RSS" as a non-goal for that epic, and calls RSS "the obvious second source later", with the tables shaped for a second intake. This story is that later source (D5).

The motivating feed was not inspected. mlarson.org was unreachable from the refinement sandbox. Whether it is RSS or Atom, and whether it carries full content, is unknown. So the spec handles RSS 2.0, Atom 1.0 and RSS 1.0/RDF, both with full content and excerpt-only.

Decision records

D1Where are feeds added? · settled

A feed is a reader_publications row with a feed_url instead of a handle. It inherits the enable toggle, the rename, the note and the summary-kind chip for free, and the publications store, routes and view already render it. Considered: a separate Feeds section, which would duplicate every one of those affordances.

D2What does a new feed backfill? · settled

Every poll considers only entries whose date (D11) is within the last seven days. That covers the first poll and a poll after re-enabling. This mirrors the newsletter worklist's horizon, so pausing and re-enabling a feed behaves exactly as it does for a sender. Considered: the newest N whatever their age; nothing older than the add.

D3Excerpt-only feeds · settled

When an entry's own content is an excerpt (D12), the leg GETs the entry's link and extracts the article (D13). It keeps whichever text is longer. If the page fetch fails, the excerpt is stored and summarised anyway, so the floor (title and link) is never lost. Considered: summarising the excerpt; filing excerpt-only entries as failed.

D4Polling cadence · settled

A feed is due when it is enabled and its feed_checked_at is null or more than an hour old. Each tick polls at most one due feed, the most overdue (null first). With twelve ticks an hour that serves twelve feeds an hour at the hourly cadence. Considered: polling every feed every tick.

D5Departing from epic §5 · proposed

Feeds become source = 'feed' on reader_posts, the fourth value, with their own identity branch. Feed summaries are model calls like any other and count toward READER_DAILY_CAP. A separate cap would be a second money knob for a single owner. The leg runs after newsletters and retries, so on a busy day mail goes first and feed entries wait (D9). Considered: leaving feeds out of the cap, which would make a misconfigured feed unbounded spend.

D6Feed health on the card · proposed

Each feed row carries its own feed_last_success_at, feed_last_error and feed_last_error_at. The card shows "checked <relative>" and, when the error is newer than the last success, the amber line in Plate I·C. The "since" time counts from the last success, as the Instapaper note does. reader_health is untouched. Considered: a header note like Instapaper's, which fits a single credential but is too loud for one dead blog among many.

D7Validate on add · proposed

POST /api/reader/publications with a feed_url fetches the URL server-side (10 s timeout, 5 MB cap) and parses it with the same parser as the Worker (D10):

  • Not XML, or no RSS/Atom root: 422 not-a-feed.
  • Network error, timeout or non-2xx: 422 unreachable, quoting the status.
  • Already on the roster (after normalisation, D14): 409.

On success the row's name is the feed's title, falling back to the host without www., and feed_checked_at is left null so the next tick polls it at once. Considered: accepting any URL and letting the first poll report, which leaves an obvious mistake undiscovered for up to five minutes on a card the owner has already left.

Implementation decisions

D8 · Schema shape

reader_publications.handle becomes nullable, and a new feed_url (unique) is added. A CHECK requires exactly one of the two. A null handle never matches v_reader_worklist's join or v_reader_candidates' not exists, and Postgres unique admits many nulls, so discovery's on conflict (handle) upsert is unaffected. "Is a feed" is always feed_url is not null. The existing source column stays provenance (auto|owner), and a feed is owner. The card shows the feed chip in place of the provenance chip.

D9 · Where the leg sits and what it costs

Order: retries → newsletters → feed leg → To Reader. The feed leg runs only when the ceiling isn't spent, a slot is free, and a feed is due. When it runs it pays three fixed fetches: the feed GET, the "already a post?" read, and the feed stamp PATCH. It pays for them by lowering the tick's post limit by one. Each new entry costs ≤ 6: page GET (only for excerpts), insert, Anthropic ×2, terminal patch. That is the same as a newsletter. Worst tick: 9 fixed + 3 To Reader + 3 feed + 5 × 6 = 45 ≤ 50. The roster read the tick already makes gains the feed columns, so choosing the due feed costs nothing. The READER_TICK_LIMIT doc table gains the feed rows and this arithmetic.

D10 · Parsing

Workers have no DOMParser. Add fast-xml-parser (pure JS, no Node built-ins) to workers and frontend. A hand-rolled regex parser would get CDATA, entities and namespaces wrong. The parse-to-entries code is one pure module per package. The route only needs "is it a feed, and what's its title", so its probe is small. The two must agree on what counts as a feed: rss/channel, feed (Atom), or rdf:RDF root.

D11 · Entry identity and date

Identity (feed_entry_id): Atom id, else RSS guid, else the entry link, else sha256(title + date). It is unique per publication. Date: Atom published ?? updated; RSS pubDate ?? dc:date. Parsed, then clamped to ≤ now, and stored as received_at (so list order and the 90-day text sweep work unchanged). An entry with no parseable date is dated at the poll instant, and so is in-horizon. To bound that, each poll considers only the 20 entries nearest the top of the document. An edited entry keeps its identity and is never re-imported.

D12 · What counts as an excerpt

The entry body is Atom content ?? summary, or RSS content:encoded ?? description, run through htmlToText. It is an excerpt when it is under 250 words, or when the only body is Atom summary / RSS description and it ends in an ellipsis or a "read more"-style link. Otherwise it is the article, and no page fetch happens.

D13 · Page extraction

The entry page is fetched only over http(s), with a 10 s timeout and a READER_HTML_CHARS cap. The main text comes from the first <article>, else <main>, else <body>, with script, style, nav, header, footer, aside, form removed, then htmlToText. If that section is longer than the feed body's text, it becomes the post's text and html, and html_extracted = true. No readability library is added: blogs that publish feeds are overwhelmingly simple templates, and the summary only needs the prose.

D14 · URL normalisation and fetch etiquette

feed_url is stored trimmed, with the scheme and host lower-cased and the fragment dropped. The path and query are kept as typed. Fetches send User-Agent: alfred-reader (+https://github.com/ac3charland/alfred), and conditional-GET with the stored feed_etag / feed_last_modified. A 304 just stamps feed_checked_at and feed_last_success_at. Redirects are followed, but the stored URL isn't rewritten.

D15 · A feed is "checked" only once it's drained

When a poll finds more new in-horizon entries than the tick's free slots, the leg takes the oldest first. It does not stamp feed_checked_at or the validators, so the feed stays due and the next tick re-polls it and takes the rest. A fully drained poll, a 304 or a failure stamps feed_checked_at. A failure also stamps the error, so a dead feed is retried hourly, not every five minutes.

Behaviour

Adding a feed (P1, D7)

  • The Add a feed caption, field and button sit between the count line and the roster. Enter submits. The button is disabled while the field is empty or a submit is in flight.
  • On success the new card appears at its sorted place (by name, as the view already sorts) and the field clears.
  • On 422 or 409 the field keeps its text, and one red line beneath it gives the reason. not-a-feed gives Plate I·B's words. unreachable gives "Couldn't reach that URL (<status or 'no answer'>)." 409 gives "That feed is already on the roster."
  • The heading's description changes to Plate I's wording. The empty-state copy mentions feeds the same way.

A feed's card (P1, D6, D8)

  • The chip reads feed, not the provenance. The sub-line reads <feed_url> · last post <date>|no posts yet · checked <relative>|not checked yet.
  • The amber line shows when feed_last_error_at > feed_last_success_at (or there is no success yet). Its text: "Couldn't read the feed: <error words>. Failing since <relative from last success, or from the first error>; retried hourly."
  • Error words: "it answered <status>", "it didn't answer", "it isn't an RSS or Atom feed any more", "it's too large to read".
  • The toggle's title for a feed: "Paused feeds aren't polled; posts already here stay. Re-enabling takes posts from the last seven days."
  • The kind chip, rename and note behave exactly as for a sender. A kind change applies to the next summary only (ALF-322).
  • Copy Gmail filter query ignores feed rows. Its disabled state counts enabled senders, so a roster of only feeds disables it with its existing tooltip. The count line counts feeds and senders alike.

The feed leg (D4, D9, D11–D15)

  1. Skip when the ceiling is spent, no slot is free, or no feed is due.
  2. Pick the most overdue due feed and conditional-GET it. A transport failure, non-2xx (≠ 304), unparsable body, or body over 5 MB: stamp the error and feed_checked_at, then end the leg. Feed failures never enter summary.failures, so they don't hold back last_success_at.
  3. Parse the entries, keep the first 20, drop those dated outside the seven-day horizon, and read which feed_entry_ids are already posts for this publication.
  4. Take the new entries, oldest first, up to the free slots. For each: build text (D12, D13), then INSERT a reader_posts row: source='feed', publication_id, feed_entry_id, title (collapsed whitespace; else "Untitled"), author (entry author ?? feed author), canonical_url (entry link if http(s)), received_at, text/html/word_count/html_extracted. A unique-violation is a lost race: skip it. Then summarise through the same summarizePost path with the publication's name and kind. Empty text → filed failed with NO_READABLE_BODY, as intake does.
  5. If every new entry was taken, stamp feed_checked_at, feed_last_success_at, feed_etag and feed_last_modified in one PATCH. Otherwise stamp nothing (D15).
  6. ReaderTickSummary gains feed?: { feedId, status: 'not-modified' | 'polled' | 'failed' | skip reason, taken, failures[] }, and logReaderTick prints it.

A feed post in the list (P2)

postEyebrow returns the linked publication's name for source='feed', falling back to UNKNOWN_PUBLICATION. No "via" suffix. Every verb (Send, Original, Overview, Archive, Retry) works unchanged. Original opens canonical_url, and a feed post with no link has no Original, the same as an Instapaper article with no URL.

Acceptance criteria

  • Pasting a valid RSS or Atom URL and pressing Add feed creates a roster card named from the feed's title, with the feed chip, Essay kind, Enabled.
  • A non-feed URL (an HTML page) shows Plate I·B's error under the field and creates nothing. An unreachable URL and a duplicate show their own lines.
  • The next tick polls a never-checked feed. A checked feed isn't polled again until an hour has passed. At most one feed is polled per tick.
  • The first poll imports only entries dated within the last seven days. Older entries never become posts.
  • Each imported entry is a reader_posts row with source='feed', summarised with its publication's kind, shown in the list as in Plate II, and counted toward the daily ceiling.
  • For an excerpt-only entry the post's text comes from the entry page's article. For a full-content entry no page is fetched.
  • Re-polling a feed never duplicates a post, including when an entry's updated date changes.
  • A feed that answers 404 shows Plate I·C's amber line on its card. The next successful poll clears it.
  • Pausing a feed stops its polling. Rename, note and kind work as on a sender card.
  • Copy Gmail filter query never includes a feed, and is disabled when no sender is enabled.

Review focus

  • A poll with more new entries than free slots → the oldest are taken, the feed stays due, and the next tick takes the rest without duplicates.
  • The entry page fails (403/timeout) → the post is stored from the excerpt and summarised, and the feed isn't marked failing.
  • Entries with no date, or a date in the future → dated at the poll instant (clamped), with at most 20 considered.
  • An entry link that's javascript: or relative → no Original link (relative links resolve against the feed URL first), never a non-http href.
  • The ceiling is spent, or newsletters fill all six slots → the leg makes no fetch at all, and the feed stays due.

Out of scope, risks & unknowns

  • Autodiscovery (homepage → <link rel="alternate">) is out: it's cheap, but it changes D7's contract. Promote it in review if wanted. Delete is out because senders can't be deleted either. Cross-source dedupe is out per epic §5: don't add a feed for a publication that also mails you.
  • Unknown: mlarson.org's format and whether it carries full content. The build session must capture it as a fixture. If its host is blocked, ask the owner to allow mlarson.org in the environment's network settings.
  • Risk: D13's <article>/<main> heuristic on unusual templates. The eval harness (npm run eval:reader) is the place to check a bad summary's source text.
  • Risk: bot-blocking hosts (Cloudflare challenges) answer 403 to Workers. That surfaces as D6's error line, which is the correct outcome.
