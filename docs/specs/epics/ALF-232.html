ALF-232 — Reader Module

Epic spec Durable context for the Reader epic: the problem space, the decisions taken and why, the architecture later stories build against, and how the work cuts into two implementation halves with a code-review checkpoint between them. Revised 2026-09-17 (first version).

One-paragraph version. The Reader is alfred's fourth module: every newsletter that already lands in the owner's personal Gmail is summarised by a model at two depths — a one-paragraph gist that answers "should I read this?", and a one-page overview that is the 80/20 alternative to reading it — and shown as a list the owner skims, opens, or archives. It is Steve Newman's reader from the Cognitive Revolution episode, built on the pipe alfred already has: the Comms module already mirrors those newsletters into the database every three minutes, so there is no interceptor, no forwarding address, no email rules to set up, and nothing to POST — the summariser is a Worker cron beside the comms classifier, and the UI reads Supabase like every other module.

1 · Problem space & why now

The owner subscribes to a lot of Substacks. They arrive in the personal Gmail inbox, they are long, most of them restate something already read that week, and the ones with a genuinely new idea are not distinguishable from the rest without opening them. The result is the one Steve Newman describes in the Cognitive Revolution episode: "I get something like 50 Substack posts, other blog posts, newsletters … per day … I was spending I don't know how many hours per day just trying to read, just keep up, let alone synthesize."

What the owner is actually buying

Steve's framing, which this epic adopts verbatim: the summary is not a substitute for the post, it is a way to decide what to read. "Is this a new take on 4.7, or is it basically the same as I've already read?" Concretely his reader has:

  • A list of posts, newest first, each carrying a precomputed one-paragraph summary readable in about ten seconds.
  • A precomputed one-page overview per post, produced by a second prompt whose sections are literally the section titles — "tell me the novel ideas here, tell me the notable evidence" — used when he is on the fence, or as the 80/20 alternative to reading.
  • Two verbs that matter: open the original, and archive. (A third, "open a Claude session with the article in context", he forgot existed — see §5.)
  • A static prompt with no personalisation context: "It's the dumbest possible thing … completely static, no context at all … I know what I've already read. I can skim a one paragraph summary in about 10 seconds and that's efficient enough."

What this epic builds on

The product spec (SPEC §13) already names this module: "An LLM reads subscribed newsletters and extracts novel insights. A reader UI lets the owner skim those points to decide what's worth reading, with a second, slightly fuller summary layer for getting the gist without reading in full. Articles flow toward knowledge." And the Comms epic (ALF-7) built the upstream half without meaning to:

  • The Worker polls the personal Gmail account every three minutes over OAuth (workers/src/comms/gmail.ts) and mirrors every inbound message into comm_messages.
  • The deterministic header filter (workers/src/comms/newsletter.ts) already stamps bulk mail with filtered_reason = 'newsletter' and records the raw has_list_header signal on every row.
  • Both alfred instances, the Worker cron, the Anthropic key, the structured-output call shape (workers/src/classifier.ts), the module shell, the store factories and the optimistic-mutation pattern all exist.

So the owner's stated constraint — "all my substacks go to my personal email; I'd prefer not to unsubscribe and resubscribe with a dedicated reader email" — is already satisfied, and the shape the ticket sketched — intercept → summarise → POST to the app, an inference from Steve's system rather than his description of it (his is an RSS reader) — collapses into read the rows that are already there → summarise → write the rows the UI reads. What the comms pipe does not give the Reader is exactly where the real work is, and each is a decision below: bodies are stripped to text and cut at 20 000 characters (D3), mirrored messages are deleted after 60 days (D3), and the comms filter's definition of "newsletter" is biased the wrong way for a reading list (D4).

2 · Decisions

Each with its why, because a decision without one gets re-litigated by the first story session that finds it inconvenient. The half that carries each decision is marked H1 / H2.

What the module is

D1The Reader is alfred's fourth module, with its own tables. A post is never an item. H1

New route group (shell)/(reader)/reader, a fourth ModuleId in frontend/lib/modules.ts, a fourth switcher segment, its own sidebar nav, its own Supabase tables (reader_*). Posts never enter the generic-item core and nothing auto-converts a post into a task or a knowledge item.

Accent: green — the existing --color-accent-green theme token (text-accent-green and friends) and the glow-green utility, no new token. SPEC §5.4 reserved green for Knowledge; the Reader is the front door to the knowledge base (SPEC §13: "articles flow toward knowledge") and no Knowledge module exists, so the hue is the lineage's, not a fifth colour. If a separate Knowledge module ever ships, the hue question reopens then.

Why: the same reason Comms got its own tables (ALF-7 D1) — the lifecycle doesn't match an item's. A post arrives, is skimmed, and is archived; it is never "done", never has a due date, never nests. Fifty rows a day through the item core would swamp every Inbox count and view the Tasks module has. Module accents are read from one table (MODULE_ACCENT), so the colour lands everywhere at once.

D2The source is the comms pipe. No interceptor, no forwarding address, no POST. H1

The Reader's intake worklist is rows already in comm_messages: inbound, on the gmail-personal account, whose sender_handle matches an enabled publication (D4), and that no reader_posts row claims yet. The Reader adds no Gmail cursor, no second OAuth client, no daemon source and no HMAC endpoint. The summariser runs as a Worker cron and writes with the service role, exactly like the comms classifier.

Why: the pipe exists and is the only part of alfred whose freshness does not depend on a Mac being awake. Steve's reader POSTs summaries into his app because his app has nothing upstream; alfred's Worker is the upstream. A second poller would mean a second cursor to keep healthy, a second place a revoked token shows up, and a second copy of every Gmail gotcha ALF-7 already paid for (the publishing-status trap, the tied-second livelock). One consequence the owner accepted knowingly: the Reader is only as fresh as the comms poll, and a Gmail outage on the comms side is a Reader outage too — the health surface (D10) says so rather than hiding it.

D3The Reader re-fetches each message and keeps its own copy of the post. H1

For each worklist row the intake calls Gmail messages.get?format=full by the row's source_id, keeps the text/html part, and extracts what a reading list needs: the title, the canonical post URL, the author, the publication name, and the full text (HTML stripped, not truncated at comms' 20 000 characters; a hard ceiling of 400 000 characters exists only against pathological mail). All of it is written to reader_posts at intake, with comm_message_id as a nullable on delete set null reference.

Why: two properties of the comms mirror make it unusable as the Reader's store. Bodies are plain text truncated to 20 000 characters (MAX_BODY_CHARS in email-text.ts) — a long Substack essay loses its tail, and the strip discards the post link and the HTML structure the title lives in. And the retention sweep deletes every mirrored message at 60 days; a reading list whose rows vanish under it would be a bug report within two months. Re-fetching costs one Gmail subrequest per post, which D9 budgets for. Email is also the better source than RSS here: a paid Substack's feed carries only the teaser, the email carries the whole post.

D4"Newsletter" means a sender on the publications roster — not what the comms filter shelved. H1 roster · H2 UI

A reader_publications table keyed on the sender handle (lower-cased address), with a display name, an enabled flag and a source of auto or owner. Two ways in:

  • Auto-enabled: a sender whose domain is substack.com, whose message carries the list header (has_list_header = true), and whose local part is not no-reply / noreply is added and enabled the first time the tick's discovery step (§3.2, step 0) sees it. The two extra conditions are what keep Substack's platform mail — comment and like notifications, digests, "Substack Reads" — from becoming "publications" at $0.03 a summary. (The owner's words: "all my substacks". Other platforms — beehiiv, Buttondown, Ghost — are candidates, not auto-enabled, until §8 Q2 says otherwise.)
  • Candidates: every other bulk sender (has_list_header = true in comm_messages, not on the roster) is listed with its message count, and the owner promotes one with a click. Half 1 seeds the roster by SQL / the route handler; the candidates list and the toggles are Half 2's publications view.

The worklist match (D2) is on the roster, regardless of the header: a publication whose mail happens to lack List-Unsubscribe still counts, and a sender on the comms people roster (which the comms filter never shelves) still counts if it is also a publication.

Why: the comms filter exists to keep obligations out of the shelf, so it is deliberately generous — it shelves Amazon promos, SaaS marketing and every mailing list alongside Substacks. A reading list wants the opposite bias: only long-form editorial content, and nothing the owner didn't choose. A roster is deterministic, inspectable, costs no model call to decide, and the candidates list means it is never a blank form to fill in — the same shape as comm_people, which the owner already maintains. Letting the model decide "is this an article?" would add a billed call per promo and a judgment nobody can audit.

D5alfred never writes to Gmail. Inbox hygiene is one Gmail filter the owner sets — the lowest-lift way to keep newsletters out of the inbox. H1 recipe · H2 helper

The Gmail token is gmail.readonly and stays so. To keep newsletters out of the inbox the owner creates one Gmail filter: From substack.com (Gmail's from: matches on domain) → Skip the Inbox (Archive it) + Apply the label "Newsletters", ticking "Also apply filter to matching conversations" for the backlog. Other publications are added as OR terms. Never "Delete it": the poll skips TRASH, so a deleted-on-arrival message never reaches alfred at all.

This is compatible with the comms poll as built: the first-run search is a bare newer_than:7d and the history walk uses historyTypes=messageAdded, neither of which is restricted to INBOX; the only labels the poller skips are SPAM, TRASH and DRAFT. An archived-on-arrival message is still mirrored, still shelved as FYI in Comms, and still summarised here. Half 1's intake story verifies this against a real filtered message rather than trusting this paragraph.

In Half 2 the publications view grows a "copy Gmail filter query" button that emits the from:(a OR b OR c) term for every enabled publication, so the two lists stay in step without alfred ever holding a write scope.

Why: ALF-7 D18 — alfred never writes to Gmail — together with D2 (alfred mirrors, it is never the store of record) is what makes the whole system safe to be wrong, and a write scope would break it for a cosmetic gain. It would also need a re-consent on a restricted scope (the ALF-7 D23 lifecycle again) and could only act up to three minutes after arrival, so the message would be in the inbox anyway. A Gmail filter acts on arrival, costs no code, and the owner already said they were open to email rules.

The summary

D6One model call per post produces both layers, as structured output. H1

The summariser makes one request per post with output_config.format set to a JSON schema (the same shape workers/src/classifier.ts uses), and stores the result as columns plus one overview JSON document:

FieldWhat it isBudget
headlineWhat the post is about, in one line. Shown as the row's second line when the title is a teaser.≤ 20 words
gistSteve's one-paragraph summary: the claim the post makes and whether it is a new take or a restatement — the "should I read this?" answer.≤ 90 words
overview.novel_ideasThe genuinely new ideas, as bullets. Empty is a valid and informative answer.0–6 bullets
overview.evidenceThe notable evidence, data, or examples the post rests on.0–6 bullets
overview.argumentThe one-page version: the argument in order, the 80/20 alternative to reading.≤ 300 words
overview.who_should_readWho the full post is worth the time for, so a skimmer can self-select.≤ 40 words

read_minutes is computed from the word count (230 wpm), not asked of the model.

Why one call: the overview and the gist come from the same reading of the same text, and two calls would pay for the input twice (the input is the whole cost here — §6). Why structured rather than prose: SPEC §13 wants the extracted insights to be what a future knowledge base indexes, so the novel ideas and the evidence are stored as arrays from day one and nothing has to re-summarise a year of posts to get them. Why these sections: they are Steve's, and they are the ones he still uses.

D7The prompt is static, versioned in code, and never re-run silently. H1 · re-run verb H2

No personalisation context, no "what I've already read", no owner-editable rubric. The prompt lives in workers/src/reader/prompt.ts with a READER_PROMPT_VERSION stamped on every summary alongside the model id. Editing the prompt re-summarises nothing; an explicit per-post re-summarise verb (Half 2) is the only way an existing summary changes.

Why: Steve tried to want the smarter version and found he didn't: "I didn't bother with that in the first iteration and I haven't been motivated to do anything about it." A rubric editor is a whole story (ALF-7 story I) for a knob nobody has asked to turn; if the owner finds themselves editing prompt.ts monthly, then it earns a UI. "Never re-run silently" is ALF-7 D19 for the same reason: a summary that changes under the owner is indistinguishable from a bug.

D8The model is Sonnet 5, as a deploy var. H1

READER_MODEL = "claude-sonnet-5" in wrangler.toml's [vars], next to CLASSIFIER_MODEL, reported by GET /. The call goes through @anthropic-ai/sdk with the same stop-reason handling classifier.ts has (max_tokens and refusal are content-shaped failures, D10) and, like classifier.ts, no extended thinking: thinking: { type: 'disabled' } (accepted on Sonnet 5) with output_config.effort medium, and max_tokens 4 096 — three times the ~1 500 tokens the D6 schema needs. Thinking is off deliberately: its tokens count against max_tokens and are billed as output, so turning it on means max_tokens ≥ 16 000, a truncation that no longer signals a bad response, and a §6 that has to be re-derived. Summarising a post is not a task where thinking earns that.

Why Sonnet, chosen by the owner in refinement: the classifier's Haiku is picked for structured-output fit at a task where every option is inside noise; "is this actually a new idea" is a synthesis task where the tier shows, and at ~$0.03 a post (§6) the step up from Haiku is a few dollars a month. Opus's extra judgment costs ~2.5× that for a difference the owner can't see in a ten-second skim. A var rather than a constant so trying another model for a week is a deploy flag, not a story.

Running it

D9Its own cron trigger, a per-tick cap, a daily ceiling, and a seven-day intake horizon. H1

A fourth trigger, READER_CRON = '*/5 * * * *', written offset-free: the cloudflare-workers skill records that a stepped range with a nonzero start (4-59/5) is normalised by the scheduler and reported in event.cron as its offset-free form, which makes the dispatch constant unreachable and every reader tick fall through to the frequent path — the exact collapse that once took the Gmail poll dark. Coinciding with */2 or */3 is harmless: each trigger is its own invocation with its own subrequest budget. One tick: discover new publications (D4), build the worklist (D2), take at most six posts oldest-first, and for each run intake (one Gmail fetch) then summarise (one model call) sequentially, writing the reader_posts row before the model call so a stalled summariser still yields a list. The worklist only considers messages received_at within the last seven days. A daily model-call ceiling, READER_DAILY_CAP (default 60, a var), is enforced from Half 1: past it, intake continues and posts sit pending until the UTC day rolls over.

Why its own trigger: the Workers runtime allows 50 outbound fetches per invocation on the Free plan, it throws part-way rather than degrading, and ALF-7 already split polling from judging because sharing a tick made each the reason the other ran out. The arithmetic, which the cap's doc comment must carry: per post up to 6 (Gmail get, Anthropic ×2 with the one retry, insert, patch, publication touch); per tick about 6 (token mint, discovery, roster, worklist, two health writes) — six posts is ≈ 42 worst case, inside 50 with room. The plan's per-Worker cron-trigger cap is not verifiable from the repo: H1-F checks it before adding the fourth trigger, and if the account refuses one, the reader tick runs on the */2 tick after the comms judge with the cap cut to two, re-doing this arithmetic against the shared budget. Why the horizon: without it, the first tick's worklist is the entire 60-day comms mirror — hundreds of posts and tens of dollars in one afternoon, most of them stale. Seven days mirrors the comms first-run window. Why the ceiling is Half 1: the per-tick cap alone allows 1 700 summaries a day; a roster mistake — a sender that mails forty times a day — must cost a capped amount from the first deploy, not from the second PR. Its banner is Half 2's; the protection is not.

D10Failures are counted the comms way, and the module has a health row. H1 mechanics · H2 surface

Per post: summary_state of pending | done | refused | failed, summarize_attempts counting content-shaped failures only (unparseable output, a schema miss, max_tokens), last_error. Transport failures are not counted and are retried next tick; at three counted attempts the post is failed and stays in the list with its title, "open original", and a marker — never dropped. A refusal stop reason is refused immediately, marked, no retry. A singleton reader_health row (last_run_at, last_success_at, last_error, last_error_at) records systemic failures — a missing var, a rejected key — and the Gmail account's own health is read from comm_accounts rather than duplicated.

Why: ALF-7 D25 learned this the hard way: counting transport failures empties an outage into a counted state. A reading list must also never lose a post to a model error — the title and the link are the floor, the summary is the value on top. Health is what makes an empty list trustworthy: "nothing new" and "the summariser died on Tuesday" must look different, and the difference is the health row plus the comms account dot.

D11Two verbs, two timestamps: open original and archive. H1

opened_at is stamped when the owner opens the original; archived_at when they archive. The list shows unarchived posts newest first; an /reader/archive view (Half 2) shows the rest. No stars, no read-later, no tags. Archiving is optimistic with rollback, through the existing optimistic-mutation.ts helper, and a row leaves with the same grid-rows collapse the comms row uses.

Why: Steve: "I can either click on it and that'll just open the original or the main thing I'll do is I'll go over here and hit archive." Everything else he built into that toolbar he stopped using. opened_at costs nothing and is the one signal a future "what do I actually read?" view would want.

D12"Open original" is the canonical post URL, with the mailbox as fallback. H1

Intake extracts the canonical URL from the HTML: the first link whose host is the publication's domain and whose path starts with /p/ (Substack's post path), else the "view in browser" link, else nothing. A post with no URL falls back to the Gmail web permalink, https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#search/rfc822msgid:<Message-ID>, built from the RFC822 id intake stores.

Why: the web post works on the phone, which is where a reading list gets skimmed. The comms module's deep link (frontend/lib/comms/deep-link.ts) is Apple Mail's message: scheme, desktop-only by ALF-7's own decision and typed on comms rows — the wrong fallback exactly where this one matters. The Gmail permalink opens in any browser (the Gmail app may claim it on the phone, which is fine). The fallback exists so the verb is never absent — a row with no way out is the one thing a reading list must not have.

D13Summaries are kept forever; full text for 90 days. H2

A retention step on the existing daily retention cron nulls reader_posts.text once a post is 90 days old. Title, URL, gist, overview, and the timestamps are never swept.

Why: at fifty posts a day of ~30 KB each, the text column grows about 1.5 MB a day — 135 MB at the 90-day mark, steady thereafter, which is inside the Supabase tier and far from it after a year. The summaries are the knowledge-base feed (SPEC §13) and are small; the text is only there to re-summarise from (D7) and nobody re-summarises a season-old post. Half 2, because nothing is 90 days old before then.

D14Comms is left exactly as it is. H1

A newsletter the Reader claims still sits on the Comms FYI shelf, still counts on the comms health strip, and is still swept by comms retention. No comms table, filter, view or store changes in this epic. One exception, and only the orchestrator makes it: an export-only change to workers/src/comms/email-text.ts — its MIME part walk, decoder and HTML stripper are module-private today — so the Reader's extractor imports them rather than copying forty lines. Behaviour unchanged, comms tests green, done in Half 1's foundations batch.

Why: the shelf is collapsed by default and is an archive being spot-checked, not a list of obligations; hiding rows from it would couple two modules' lifecycles for a gain nobody has asked for, and would make the comms "did the filter shelve this?" audit lie. If the shelf becomes noise once the Reader exists, that is a one-line Comms story later.

D15Two implementation halves, each one orchestrator session and one PR, with a code review between. H1H2

The epic ships in two phase: epic-implementation PRs. Half 1 proves the pipe end to end and gives the owner a usable list on the phone. Half 2 makes it operable: the publications UI, the health surface, the cost guardrails, the archive, the keyboard. The review between them is a real checkpoint: Half 2 starts from main after Half 1 has merged, never from Half 1's branch. §7 is the plan for each half, including which subagents build what.

Why: the owner asked for it, and the cut is where the risk changes shape. Half 1's risk is "does this extraction and this prompt work on real Substack mail?" — a question best answered with a week of real posts before the operability layer is built on top of the answer. Half 2's risk is integration polish, which is cheap to review when the pipe underneath it is already trusted.

3 · Architecture

The Reader adds the bottom row. Everything in the top row already runs; the dashed box is the owner's, not alfred's.

3.1 Data model

One migration, database/migrations/<next>_reader.sql — take the next free number at build time, never pin one here. Same RLS and grant pattern as 0034_comms.sql: the browser reads and writes triage state as the authenticated owner; the Worker writes posts, summaries and health with the service role. migration-lint wants explicit sequence and view grants.

3.2 The tick

runReaderTick(env, now):
  health.last_run_at = now                                                (1 write)
  0 discover: distinct inbound substack.com senders with the list header
     in the last 7d that are not on the roster → upsert as enabled (D4)   (1 query + 1 batch upsert)
  1 worklist = select * from v_reader_worklist limit 6                    (1 query; §3.1)
     plus: pending rows from earlier ticks with attempts < 3, oldest first, ahead of new ones
  2 for each, sequentially, while calls_today < READER_DAILY_CAP:
     full   = gmail.messages.get(source_id, format=full)                 (1; a transport failure ends the tick —
                                                                            nothing is advanced, the row is retried next tick)
     post   = extract(full) → title, author, canonical_url, html→text, word_count
     row    = insert reader_posts (summary_state = 'pending')            (1; dedupe on account_key + gmail_message_id)
     result = summarizePost(row)                                          (1, +1 on the single retry; D6 schema, D8 model)
     patch  = done | refused | counted failure | uncounted failure        (1; D10)
     calls_today += 1
  health.last_success_at = now  (only when the tick finished without a systemic error)   (1 write)

The intake insert happens before the model call on purpose (D9): the title and the link are the floor. A tick that dies mid-way leaves pending rows the next tick picks up, never a half-written summary.

3.3 What the model is asked for

System prompt (static, cached): the owner reads a lot of newsletters and uses this to decide what to read; summarise this post only; be concrete; "novel" means against what a well-read person in the field already knows, and an empty list is the honest answer for a post that restates the week's consensus; do not pad; do not editorialise about quality beyond the "should I read this" verdict. The user turn is the post metadata (publication, author, title, date) and the full text. The response is constrained to the D6 schema. The prompt story owns the exact wording and iterates it against a fixture set of real posts (see §7, H1-C).

3.4 Contracts the slices build to

Pinned here so parallel subagents build to one seam instead of inventing two (§7). These are the shape of each seam; the identifiers are suggestions the orchestrator confirms or renames at dispatch time, and the build's names win — this document does not chase them (the implement-epic skill forbids editing it). What is not negotiable is the shape: one store, one data function per read, a list payload that never carries text, one dedupe key.

5 · Constraints & non-goals

  • No new source. Email via the comms mirror is the only intake. No RSS, no podcasts, no Twitter, no "paste a URL". RSS is the obvious second source later, and the tables are shaped so it would be a second intake writing the same reader_posts row (the dedupe key would grow a source discriminator) — but it is not this epic.
  • No write scope on Gmail (D5). No labelling, archiving or deleting mail from alfred.
  • No personalisation, no "what have I already read", no cross-post deduplication (D7). Steve's answer to Nathan's question about context is the design: none.
  • No prompt editor, no rubric, no corrections-as-examples. The comms machinery for learning from corrections has no analogue here because there is no verdict to correct.
  • No "open in Claude with the post in context". Steve built it and forgot it existed.
  • No knowledge base. The overview is stored in the shape a knowledge module would index (D6) and nothing indexes it.
  • No Comms changes (D14).
  • No backfill beyond seven days (D9). If the owner wants a month of history summarised, that is a deliberate, separately budgeted act.
  • No push, no notifications, no daily digest email. The list is pulled when the owner has idle time, which is exactly how Steve uses his.
  • Desktop and phone both, like every module: the reading list is skimmed on the phone.

6 · What it costs to run

The whole cost is input tokens, and input is the post. Assume ~8 000 input tokens and ~1 500 output tokens per post (thinking is off, D8, so output is the schema and nothing else) at Anthropic's list prices at the time of writing (Sonnet 5 $2 / $10 per million, Opus 5 $5 / $25, Haiku 4.5 $1 / $5); the static system prompt is cached and is a rounding error.

7 · How the work splits: two halves, and the subagent plan for each

Which half am I building? Each half is one implement-epic orchestrator session and one PR carrying phase: epic-implementation for ALF-232. The launch prompt names the half; if it doesn't, ask before dispatching anything. Half 2 starts from main after Half 1's PR has been reviewed and merged — the review is the checkpoint, and it is where the extraction and the prompt get judged against a week of real posts before anything is built on them. The implement-epic skill says "the whole spec is in scope"; this section narrows that to the half you were given. Do not pull Half 2 slices into Half 1 because they look small.

The orchestration rules are the implement-epic skill's — foundations first and alone, vertical slices, one file boundary per subagent, parallel only when disjoint, the orchestrator owns integration and runs the full check — applied here to a concrete plan. The seams in §3.4 are what let the batches run in parallel: every subagent builds to the pinned contract rather than to a neighbour's unfinished file.

The epic-refinement skill says an epic spec is not a per-file plan, and it is right in general; the owner asked for this level of detail so two orchestrator sessions cut the same way. Read the tables below as the orchestrator's starting cut, made against the code as it was when this was written: re-cut against the code in front of you, keep the batch boundaries and the ownership rule, and treat every filename as a suggestion the build may rename. 8 · Open questions

Genuinely open. Everything that could be settled in refinement was, above.

Q1 — What is the actual daily volume?

Every cost and cap number above assumes 20–50 posts a day, which is Steve's number, not the owner's. The comms mirror can answer this before Half 1 starts: select sender_handle, count(*) from comm_messages where sender_handle like '%@substack.com' and has_list_header and received_at > now() - interval '30 days' group by 1 order by 2 desc — the roster's population, not the comms filter's (which is D4's complaint: it includes Amazon). If the answer is 5, the daily ceiling and the per-tick cap are generous and could be dropped; if it is 100, Sonnet's bill is ~$95 a month and D8 deserves a second look. Owner: the Half 1 orchestrator, first thing, recorded in the demo doc.

Q2 — Which platforms auto-enable besides Substack?

D4 auto-enables substack.com only. beehiiv (*.beehiiv.com), Buttondown, Ghost and Kit senders are candidates the owner promotes. If the owner's list turns out to be mostly Substack this stays; if half the reading is elsewhere, the auto-enable domain list grows in Half 2's publications story. Nothing is blocked on it.

Q3 — Does the Gmail filter's from: match every Substack?

Substack mails from <publication>@substack.com, which from:substack.com catches. Some publications on custom domains (Stratechery, some Ghost sites) need their own term — which is what Half 2's "copy filter query" button is for. Open only in the sense that the owner should look at the roster after a week and add the stragglers.

Q4 — Should the FYI shelf hide reader-claimed posts eventually?

D14 says no for this epic. The owner will know within a month whether the shelf became noise; if so it is a one-line Comms story ("shelf excludes rows with a reader_posts match"), not a reason to reopen D14 here.
