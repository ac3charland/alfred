ALF-312 — Would medium have done the stories I ran at Extra?

Yes. Replayed fresh, both stories you had run at Extra came out correct and gate-green at medium, for 42% and 63% of Extra’s cost. Extra bought breadth, not correctness.

Where we landed

Launch Opus 5.5 implementation and bug stories at medium, and pick Extra only for a reason you can name. Medium found ALF-317’s real cause and passed the original fix’s regression test. It fixed only the column the report named, though, and filed the same-cause siblings as a follow-up. On ALF-238, four blind judges split two-two. Don’t run Opus 5: at Extra it was no better than 5.5 medium, at four to five times the cost. Two replays are a case study, not a rate. Next I’d cut the effort trial ALF-283 proposed: medium against high, assigned by ticket-ref hash on the implementation and bug lanes, and pinned through a cloud environment per level.

The evidence

Everything below is for whoever builds on this. Acting on the answer needs only what’s above.

The experiment

The question, as the owner sharpened it in the first attempt at this spike: take a session that ran at Extra (xhigh) and see whether it could have been completed successfully at Medium. The owner then settled three design questions before anything ran:

  • Stories. Two: a bug and a story built from a spec, so two lanes are covered rather than one anecdote.
  • Branches. Arms push to alf-312/* branches and open no PRs.
  • Opus 5. Add an Opus 5 xhigh arm, to test the premise that Opus 5 at Extra equals Opus 5.5 at Medium.

Medium against Extra on Opus 5.5:

  • ALF-317: 42% of the cost, 36% of the output, 60% of the time.
  • ALF-238: 63% of the cost and 50% of the output, but it ran 22 minutes longer. The Extra arm sent two slices to Sonnet subagents in parallel (the Worker change and the Instapaper client) and reviewed their work. Medium did everything itself. So effort changed how the agent organised the work, not only how long it thought.

Cache reads track output: an arm that thinks more takes more turns, and every turn re-reads the context. That is why the cost ratio follows the output ratio rather than the price list.

Run-to-run noise

The fresh xhigh replays cost 14% (ALF-317) and 31% (ALF-238) more than the original xhigh sessions. The originals differ in more than chance: owner turns, a rebase, another environment, an older CLI. Still, that is the size of variation to expect at one setting. ALF-317’s 58% saving is well outside it. ALF-238’s 37% is not.

ALF-317: did medium find the bug?

Yes. All three arms reproduced the defect before changing anything, and ruled out the realtime path with a contrast test. All three named the same root cause: codeStoryStatusPatch in frontend/lib/code/status.ts. That is the projection the navigation refetch (refreshStatuses) applies, and it carried factory_state but not spec_path. So a story that reached Ready for Dev through the refetch launched the skip-refinement prompt. This is the cause the original session and its reviewer found.

Review rounds and gates

  • All three reviewers said “ship it”. Medium’s raised 4 findings (2 fixed, 1 raised, 1 question). Extra’s raised 6 (4 fixed, 2 raised). Opus 5’s raised 5 (3 fixed, 1 raised, 1 declined after a probe proved its premise false).
  • Every push passed the pre-push check:slow: 283 Storybook snapshots, 352 Playwright tests and 121 database integration tests.
  • Demo screenshots: Extra 7, Opus 5 5, medium 2. Extra and Opus 5 each recorded two skill gotchas. Extra also admits one npx jest run, against CLAUDE.md.

ALF-238: medium against Extra on a story

Both Opus 5.5 arms built every layer the spec lists and archived the spec. Both pushed through every gate: Storybook (216–217 snapshots), Playwright (263–264), and database integration (82/82). Each reported nothing in the spec left unbuilt. The diffs judged are 60 files each: +2,987 lines for medium and +4,536 for Extra, most of the difference being tests.

Blind judges

The procedure was the llm-evaluation skill’s replay judging. Diffs were masked, binary snapshots, generated types, skill notes and the date-rot fix were excluded, and patch files had neutral names. Each judge graded a 28–31-item checklist from the spec with file:line citations, hunted defects, scored five criteria, then gave a verdict. A tie on fidelity and correctness was to go to the smaller patch.

What separated them

All of these I checked in the patches:

  • Extra, better: it applies the spec’s outcome table as written, checking HTTP 401/403 and 5xx before the body’s error code. Medium reads the body first, so a 401 carrying an error code maps to 422 instead of 502.
  • Extra, better: a 375 px Playwright test asserts the three verbs share one line with no overflow. Medium has a Storybook snapshot and no assertion.
  • Extra, better: its OAuth test reproduces a published signature end to end through signRequest, and every outcome row is exercised at route level.
  • Medium, better: its migration ends with notify pgrst, 'reload schema', like the column-adding migrations before it. Extra’s omits it.
  • Medium, better: markReaderPostSent uses .maybeSingle(), as lib/data/reader.ts documents, so a vanished row is a 404. Extra uses .single(), which makes it a 500.
  • Both: the same date-rotted habits E2E (June 2026 seeds now outside the 120-day window) failed the first push. Each re-anchored it in a separate commit. That is an artefact of the old base, not a finding about effort.

Opus 5 at Extra against Opus 5.5 at medium

The premise

The ticket says Anthropic’s release makes Opus 5 at Extra equal to Opus 5.5 at medium. The release (2026-09-22) claims something close, but against high:

  • “Opus 5.5 at default effort matched Opus 5 at high effort on half the output.”
  • On CursorBench, 5.5 at medium scores 52.5% against 46.6% for Opus 5 at max.

Anthropic’s migration guide adds that effort names don’t carry across models. At a given level, Opus 5.5 thinks more per turn than Opus 5, especially at xhigh. The premise is therefore roughly the release’s claim, stated one level higher.

What happened

  • ALF-317: Opus 5 at Extra was correct, passed the hidden test, and was broader than 5.5 medium (3 of 5 columns against 1). It cost $22.71, 4.8× medium’s $4.71 and 2.0× 5.5 at Extra.
  • ALF-238: Opus 5 at Extra built every layer and passed every gate, at $67.83, 4.4× medium’s cost. All four blind judges preferred 5.5 medium’s build, two at medium confidence, holding across both orders and both judge families (table below).

The reasons the judges agreed on, each checked in the patch:

  • Opus 5 has no evidence for the 375 px criterion. It has no story, test or demo image; medium has a 343 px story.
  • Its xAuth token exchange signs with an empty oauth_token, where RFC 5849 §3.1 says to omit it, and the exchange is untested.
  • Its provider prop is optional and defaults to “configured”. A caller that forgets it therefore shows an enabled Send that answers 501.

Opus 5 had the broader tests in places, such as an i-key E2E and route-level outcome rows. One of its judges also noted it follows the repo’s Stryker test-environment pragma, which medium doesn’t.

On quality, 5.5 medium matched Opus 5 at Extra on the bug and beat it on the story. On cost the comparison isn’t close:

  • ALF-317: the premium is price alone. Opus 5 at xhigh spent almost exactly the tokens 5.5 at xhigh did: 112k output and 32.5M cache reads, against 113k and 31.7M. Opus 5 costs $5 / $25 per million against $4 / $20, and its cache reads about $0.50 against $0.20. Repriced at Opus 5.5 rates, its tokens come to about $12, which is 5.5 Extra’s bill.
  • ALF-238: price and turns compound. Opus 5 delegated nothing, ran longest, and read the most context: 113M cache reads, against 76M for 5.5 Extra and 49M for medium. That is about $32 at 5.5 rates and $67.83 at its own.

Cache reads dominate long sessions, so Opus 5’s 2.5× cache price decides the bill.

Facts behind the table

  • Subagents inherit the session’s effort, a model: override included. Each arm’s Opus reviewer ran at the arm’s level. ALF-238 Extra’s two Sonnet subagents ran at xhigh too, and so did this session’s own judges, because the spike itself ran at xhigh.
  • Check what ran in the transcript. Every assistant entry, main and subagents/*.jsonl, has an effort field; $CLAUDE_EFFORT in the shell agrees. get_session’s session_context.effort_level shows only a picker or /effort choice. It is absent under the settings route.
  • effortLevel accepts low to xhigh, not max (the CLI’s settings schema). Never commit it to main: it would apply to every session started on that commit. Whether it beats a claude.ai picker choice is untested.
  • The cloud session-create API does take config.effort_level; that is how the picker reaches the session. The create_session tool doesn’t expose it.
  • Launch deep links document only prompt, prompt_url, repositories and environment. For alfred’s launcher to assign effort, which an effort trial needs, the only route is an environment per level carrying CLAUDE_CODE_EFFORT_LEVEL, chosen with environment=. That route is the one not yet run in the cloud.
  • The CLI’s schema has more effort controls, none tested here: modelSettings.<model>.effortLevel; maxEffortLevel, which clamps every other route, the environment variable included; and an effort: field in agent-definition and skill frontmatter.

Other things this turned up

  • You had already moved off Extra. Of 73 Opus 5.5 sessions on alfred since the model shipped on 2026-09-22, 31 ran at xhigh, 34 at high and 8 at medium. Since 2026-10-02 it is 3, 12 and 5 of 20. The open question is high against medium more than Extra against medium.
  • Old bases rot. A test with hard-coded dates on a nine-day-old base failed every ALF-238 arm’s pre-push gate. The replay guide now says to expect it and to leave that commit out of comparisons.
  • git update-ref beats fetching. Pointing origin/main at the base satisfied the pre-push gate in all six arms without fetching the merged solution. The replay guide now says this, replacing its earlier “fetch for the gate only”.
  • Six arms in parallel raised the 5-hour rate-limit window to allowed_warning. Nothing stalled.
  • The arms obeyed the replay rules. None opened a PR (a search for head:alf-312 returns nothing), and every one reported pointing origin/main at its base rather than fetching. I didn’t audit their transcripts for reads of the answer.

How much to trust it

  • n = 1 per arm per story. This is a case study, as the llm-evaluation skill warns. The ALF-317 cost gap is larger than the observed run-to-run noise. The ALF-238 gap isn’t, and ALF-238’s quality judgement is a split.
  • The breadth gap may be effort, prompt or chance. Both Opus 5.5 Extra runs, original and replay, fixed all five columns, and medium fixed one. That fits effort, but it is two runs against one, and the prompt’s “raise adjacent bugs” clause invites either reading.
  • No human in the loop. The originals had owner turns. These ran unattended, which is the condition the launch prompts are written for, but not how they were used.
  • The judges read diffs, not running software. They were controlled for order and model family, and their key claims were checked against the patches. Both arms also passed the same real gates.
  • Two lanes only. Spec writing (refinement) and spikes weren’t tested. That is where most of your remaining Extra launches go, and no gate checks their output.

Alternatives we’re not taking

Replay ALF-289 again

The first attempt at this spike proposed it, reusing ALF-265’s medium arm. The owner preferred a story that had actually run at Extra.

Make Extra the default “to be safe”

On these two stories it raised cost 1.6–2.4× for breadth that a follow-up story or a sharper prompt can buy more cheaply. Its own slips (a missing schema reload, a 500 path) show it isn’t a safety margin either.

Pin effort with /effort for the arms

It works, but it moves the task into the system prompt and spoils the arm’s ledger prompt. The settings route keeps the launch prompt as the first turn and needs no owner setup.

More replays now, for an interval

Each story’s three arms cost $39–$107 together, plus judging. ALF-283’s power figures put a decision at dozens of sessions per arm. The ledger’s randomised effort trial gets those for free from ordinary launches.

Cost & open questions

  • What this spike spent. Probes $2.35. Arms $145.93 (ALF-317 $38.71, ALF-238 $107.22). Four judges used 338k–363k subagent tokens each, and four more on Opus 5 against medium used 323k–351k each. On top of that, this session’s own cost.
  • Medium or high? High is where you launch most stories now, and nothing here measured it. That is the trial’s job.
  • Does CLAUDE_CODE_EFFORT_LEVEL work in a cloud environment, and does it override the claude.ai picker? The launcher-driven trial depends on the first. The second is a trap if it does, because an environment would silently overrule your pick.
  • Does effortLevel in settings beat the picker? Untested. Keep it on throwaway branches.
  • Refinement and spikes at medium. Untested. Specs are judged by humans, so a replay would need a blind spec judge.
